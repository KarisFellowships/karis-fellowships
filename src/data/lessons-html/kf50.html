0:08 MINDFULNESS & MEDITATION TRAINING

KF Confession 12 is the heartbeat of our redeemed life in Christ:

We circulate our true resources, reconnecting to our Creator and to the Creation.

We are approaching the end of the Karis Fellowships training cycle. You might like to review the meditation and mindfulness methods that we have practiced and choose one that works for you. Pursue your meditation practice diligently. You will gain the greatest benefits if you practice for at least twenty minutes in the morning and twenty minutes later in the day. You may wish to practice the same method for one year, or you may wish to practice the method offered in the lesson. It’s your meditation practice. Do whatever works for you.

This month we will practice a meditation called Waiting on the Lord. This meditation blends easily with other meditation practices or it can stand alone. In this lesson, we will briefly prepare using Alternate Nostril Breathing and the Sunlight Meditation.

Take a meditation posture. Straighten your neck and lower your chin a little. Imagine that your spine is like a radio tower, with the pole of your head reaching toward the heavens and your tailbone reaching toward the earth. Gently close your eyes and begin with two minutes of Alternate Nostril Breathing. (Pause for 2 minutes)

Pause, and allow your shoulders to drop back into a relaxed position. Scan and observe your body. Do you notice any tightness or pain? Breathe, and release with the exhale any tension in your body. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Now, with eyelids gently closed, lift your eyes and imagine the sun or a lamp shining onto your forehead. Breathe. Imagine that you are looking through your forehead into the light and that the light is pouring into your face as you breathe. (Pause for 1 minute)

At some point, perhaps later if not right now, you might imagine that you are gazing into the face of Jesus or into the glory of God. For one minute, just breathe and gaze while you wait before the Lord. (Pause for 1 minute)

Pause, and gently open your eyes. Take a deep breath and return to the meeting.

Our hymn for this meditation is Be Thou My Vision.

Be Thou my Wisdom and Thou my true Word,

I ever with Thee and Thou with me, Lord;

Thou my great Father, I Thy true son;

Thou in me dwelling, and I with Thee one.[1]

When you practice this meditation, you might also consider Isaiah 40:31.

But those who wait on the Lord shall renew their strength;

They shall mount up with wings like eagles;

They shall run and not be weary;

They shall walk and not faint.

God promises these great benefits to those who learn to wait on Him.

Take a deep breath.

0:21 TOOLBOX

Let’s listen again to our final KF Confession of purpose and mission:

We circulate our true resources, reconnecting to our Creator and to the Creation.

Last month, we talked about taking responsibility for the damage we have done. Now, we can learn to co‑labor with God to redeem our debts and give back what we have stolen. We can also give karis gifts that help repair damage we did not cause. By circulating gifts, we help God heal not only ourselves but also this world.

What does it mean to circulate gifts? The gifts that we give come out of the storehouse of our true resources. God designed each of us to have our own supply of resources, but in our misorientation we refuse to access and develop them. Through our efforts toward orientation, we discover that we have much more to give than we realized. The Idol cannot be generous with its resources. It is not self-sustaining, nor is it connected to the Creation, so it must crave and snatch to support itself. Our True Self, however, flows in harmony and correspondence with the Creation, giving and receiving as naturally as we breathe and as the tide flows. When we are oriented, we let go in order to receive, and in this way we nourish and circulate our own life force. Just as our heart pumps blood through our bodies, so we pump the energetic flow of the Creation; as a result, we thrive. We are the heart that keeps the Creation circulating, so the moment that we hoard or grab, the heart of the Creation stops beating.

It takes effort and training to learn to pump efficiently and with joy, so particularly at the beginning of your work, you might feel like you are making a lot of effort but only gaining small benefits. In past lessons we talked about damage in terms of debt. If your immediate outcomes seem like a poor return on the hard work that you are investing, you are simply experiencing the evidence that you are still deep in debt. The deeper you are in debt, the longer it will take to produce discernible, oriented results. It takes time and a great deal of effort just to begin the process of orienting your life, and it takes even more time and effort, as well as developed CORE muscles, to effect beneficial changes in the material world. As you continue to be courageous and oriented, live in reality, and practice endurance, you will see increasing evidence of powerful and positive change in your life.

Whenever we try to use our newly acquired skills and benefits to actualize the unfulfilled dreams of our idealized glory, energetic circulation stagnates. If, however, we focus on the next step in the dance of life, forgetting all that we have formerly relied upon to support our pride, we are carried along in the harmonious and oriented flow of Creation. With our hearts set on winning the race, we press forward to discover our potential in Christ.[2] Our True Self grows stronger when we circulate today, and it atrophies when we are satisfied with our stories of yesterday’s generosity, miracles, or judgments. Our natural habitat is a place of circulation where we receive and let go, today, in the flow.[3]

The flow of karis circulation is exactly like dancing with a partner. If you have ever tried to learn the waltz, you know that the first steps can feel awkward. When the music starts flowing, you can barely keep up. You courageously endure the shame of stepping on someone else’s toes and the pain of presenting your own toes to be stepped on. Then, after a great deal of practice, you suddenly catch the music and are able to dance. You feel exquisite joy over your brief accomplishment and are momentarily distracted by euphoria. You might even stop dancing so that you can take some pride in your accomplishment, but in doing so, you disconnect from the present moment. The music flows on, but you have stopped dancing. You have allowed prideful attachment to your achievements or fear of failure to interfere with the next step. You cannot cling to old things and, at the same time, flow into new opportunities. Your goal is to enjoy the present moment of harmony and then allow it to disappear into the joy of the next moment of harmony. With practice, your whole life becomes a series of joyful dance steps that follow the music of the flow. As you allow one step to pass, you turn your attention to the next step and experience the unique harmony that it brings.[4] There is no hoarding or clinging; if you want to stay in the dance, you have to let go. You have to let go of former glory, and you also have to forgive yourself for not making the grade. If, however, you are struggling to let go of the pride of past achievements and the self-hate of former failures, be compassionate toward yourself. It takes time and effort to learn new skills and to train your CORE muscles so that you can dance.

Whenever you use energy to nurse resentment and maintain grudges, you are hoarding energy that is intended to flow. Forgiveness releases misoriented energy and circulates a gift. Jesus told us to bless our enemies rather than resenting them because He wants us to gain the benefits of circulation. When you forgive, you allow misoriented energy to go back to whoever is responsible for it. You are then free to joyfully embrace your next step.

Our prayer might go something like this:

Great Teacher of the flow, I want to learn how to dance. (Repeat)

1:00 BIBLE TEACHING

KF Confession 12 is the law of Christ’s kingdom and our path to real glory:

We circulate our true resources, reconnecting to our Creator and to the Creation.

Let’s look at Nancy’s translation of Luke 6:31–38. In this passage, Jesus is teaching His followers the principle of karis circulation.

31And just as you want people to do to you, do the same to them. 32If you show agape love to those who love you, what karis is that to you? Look at it this way: even misoriented people love those who love them. 33And if you do good to those who do good to you, what karis is it to you? Even misoriented people do that. 34And if you lend to those from whom you hope or expect to receive, what karis is it to you? Even the misoriented lend to the misoriented in order to receive the same things in return. 35Instead, love your enemies and do good and lend, expecting nothing from them in return. Then your reward will be abundant, and you will be revealed as the sons of the Most High, your Father, because He does active good and is kind to those who are hurtful and against karis. 36Become merciful, just as your Father is merciful and has pity. 37Do not judge, and you will in no way be judged. Also, do not condemn, and you will not be condemned. Release, and you will be released. 38Give, and others will give into your open arms a true measure, already pressed down, already shaken and running over. This is how it works: in whatever proportion you measure out your resources, the same proportion will be measured back to you.

Take a deep breath.

Jesus begins His teaching about karis by reminding His disciples of what is often called the Golden Rule: “Do for others what you want them to do for you.”[5] Give in proportion to your MATH­—your money, abilities, time, and health. Learn to open your arms, and then watch for the response from others, from the Creation, and from God. Learn to trust.

Karis is more than being kind to those who have already shown kindness to you. Karis requires the ingredient of trust. If you show kindness to those who have shown kindness to you, you do not have to trust them to return a benefit; you have already received a benefit. Similarly, if you lend or give to another person with the expectation of a particular return, you are not in a trusting karis relationship; you are in a contract based on the law of mutual expectations. Contracts are necessary in the market economy, and people set them up because they don’t trust each other. A contract is intended to bind another person and exact a premeditated return. If you do something for someone else and then feel disappointed with their response, you know that you have an expectation. You are not in karis; you are in a contract.

Oriented people look forward to what returns to them in karis circulation. They confidently anticipate that God and others will respond with invitations to build relationships of deeper trust, leading to greater opportunities. Can you see how increased trust brings more freedom, not only to you but also to the whole community? Poised for the next joyful step, oriented people continue to practice A-S-A-P—Ask, Simplify, Adventure, Prioritize. Every gift they give is an adventure and an exploration of gratitude. They throw their gift into the flow and then watch to see what happens.

Karis relationships are built on trust and mutual generosity. God invites you into karis relationship by giving you a great gift—the restoration of your soul—and by returning a gift, you invite God into more relationship. If you want a karis relationship with anyone, you have to respond with gifts that are proportionate to your MATH and to your gratitude. Your response is a gift that says, “I value my relationship with you, not the return I expect from you.” When you see how others respond, you learn a lot about the quality of your relationships, and you find out how much others value you.

Whenever you give out of karis, you go into the not‑knowing. You can always trust that there will be a response to your gift, but you cannot know what that response will be, and in karis you do not attach to an expectation of what it should be. When gifts are returned, you become so rich within your True Self and in your relationship with God and others that you would rather bless your enemies than hold grudges against them.[6] Forgiveness and letting go become natural and easy in the rich flow of circulation. Holding a grudge is like refusing to dance because someone has stepped on your toe. Oriented people would rather dance.

Jesus is showing you in this passage that you are too rich to live like those who demand that others reciprocate in a certain way. Misoriented people can only go as far as exchanging mutual expectations. Their demands are based on law and contract, not on karis. Contracts and law are based on distrust; the purpose of karis, however, is to build trust. All of us are afraid to trust, and that makes karis a lot scarier and more challenging, but only karis can reveal the glory of God and the real glory of you. In karis, we give because of who we are—the sons of the Most High, who have an inheritance in this world and in the next. We give to see what will happen, knowing that all of our giving is ultimately a gift to God in karis response to all that God has given to us.

Our prayer might go something like this:

Lord Jesus, teach me to let go and get in the flow. (Repeat)

Take a deep breath.

1:10 INTEGRATION

Sip some purified water and prepare to take a few notes. Assume a meditation posture, and breathe. (Pause for 10 seconds)

Here is our integration focus: Consider letting go of something, perhaps a grudge or a gift. (Repeat) (Pause for 30 seconds)

The FAITH Process

Feelings

As you consider letting go of something, perhaps a grudge or a gift, how does it feel? (Pause) Your feelings are yours to teach you about yourself, to guide you, and to train you. They can often be uncomfortable. (Pause) Recognize with compassion whatever you are experiencing, and breathe. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Awareness without judgment

Gently hold in your awareness whatever you may be experiencing when you consider letting go. You might notice a compulsion to justify or to simply dismiss thoughts and feelings. Try to hold your experience up to the light and mindfully observe whatever you may be experiencing. Breathe. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Inquiry: What is my body saying?

Breathe, and bring your attention to your body. Notice what it is doing as you consider letting go of something, perhaps a grudge or a gift. What sensations are there? (Pause) Your body is always talking to you. You may not understand its language right now, but you can observe. For example, you might recognize what greed, offended pride, fear, or release feels like in your body. Breathe. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Talk to Jesus

You might imagine that you are leaning back in the safe and loving arms of Jesus as together you consider letting go of something. Does that feel comfortable or uncomfortable, or are you shutting down? Just observe. (Pause) Does the thought of letting go of this thing feel good or bad? (Pause) What are your reasons? What story do you want to tell? (Pause) What do you fear or hope for? Observe your responses as just information, and breathe with a long exhaling breath. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Heal

As you breathe, repeat the word Welcome. (Pause for 10 seconds)

(Pause for 8 seconds or one long breath after each statement)

  • Welcome this opportunity to explore what you might let go.
  • Welcome the gentle Holy Breath of truth, who wants to teach you how to circulate.
  • Welcome your present reality and the progress you have made.
  • Welcome what you are learning about yourself. (Pause for 10 seconds)

Now, as you breathe, you might use the following statements as a confession to the Lord and turn away from the Idol Self. On the exhale, let go.[7]

(Pause for 5 seconds after each statement)

  • As I learn to circulate, I let go of the craving to hoard and control.
  • As I learn to circulate, I let go of the craving to support and defend myself.
  • As I learn to circulate, I let go of the craving for security and self-sufficiency.
  • As I learn to circulate, I let go of the craving for idealized glory. (Pause for 30 seconds)

(Optional) Continue to breathe while I test for further integration.

Pause. Take a deep breath.
