0:07 GUIDELINES & LIMITATIONS

Let’s look at the first KF guideline: “We cooperate for the security and efficiency of the group.” Participating members cooperate to ensure a secure environment and an efficient experience for everyone by making a trustworthy effort to attend the entire meeting. By walking together through all the steps of the training, we lay the necessary foundation for the integration process and approach our healing as one entity.

(Optional) Our souls need congruence; therefore, we cannot say one thing and do another without harming ourselves. In order to maintain this inner balance and integrity, we have to do what we say. We will now do our best to begin our meetings on time. This may seem rigid to some, but our meeting guidelines remind us that, as creatures living in time, we choose to honor time limitations. Our Idol Self will rebel against limitations, and some of us are not yet strong enough to defy the Idol Self. 0:08 MINDFULNESS & MEDITATION TRAINING

Through mindfulness training, you learn that you don’t have to judge or even speak when situations arise. During the next few minutes, you can quiet your mind and stop the chatter of the Idol Self.

You are training to be still in your spirit and to observe without judgment while you learn to allow whatever is happening to just be. During this mindfulness exercise, refuse to evaluate the performance of anyone, including yourself. Observe where your thoughts want to scatter and then gently draw them back to the breath. Take it easy and be kind to yourself in this learning process.

The 3-Minute Breathing Space[1]

AWARENESS

Assume a mindfulness posture, and breathe. (Pause) Get to know yourself in this moment, in this space. Explore your current experience. (Pause) You might notice sensations in your body, or you might be aware of passing thoughts and feelings. You may find these comfortable, or you may not. Experiment with simply observing and acknowledging what is there. Just allow it to be, and observe. (Pause for 30 seconds)

GATHERING

Gently bring your full attention to your breath, watching each in-breath and each out-breath as you breathe naturally. Where do you notice your breathing? (Pause) Feel your body move as it breathes. When your mind wanders, gently guide it back to your breath. (Pause for 30 seconds)

EXPANDING

Expand your awareness now to include a sense of yourself as a whole person. As you breathe, feel your whole body respond to the inhale and the exhale. Observe your thoughts and feelings. Within your mind, connect your breath to your body, and connect your body to your breath. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Close this time by gradually re-engaging with the world around you. Open your eyes. You may want to stretch, breathe deeply, or even yawn. Experiment with allowing this connection with yourself to be your companion throughout the rest of the meeting.

During the week, you might notice how the breath is used in the Bible and in our culture.

Tapping—when the going gets rough

Tapping is one of the tools that we use in KF to train hard so that we can fight easy when dealing with our weaknesses and fears. Stored information in our bodies drives us to react before we can think, so we work with our bodies first. With calmer bodies, we have more freedom to choose healthier outcomes.

I hope that you were able to download the Tapping Protocol from the Karis Fellowships website. You can also watch Alison’s tapping and TAT videos which demonstrate this tool. While I read, follow along in your notes and participate as best you can.

The Tapping Protocol[5]

Before you begin tapping, you need to formulate your healing statement and write it down. This statement describes the problem that triggered your stress reaction and, also, identifies a few of the emotions that you are experiencing. Allow yourself to play with this a little. The statement does not have to be perfectly crafted. Here is an example of a healing statement: My problem is that I am experiencing a life change, and when I think of that, I feel lonely, distressed, anxious, and vulnerable.

Now let’s go step by step through the process of creating a personal healing statement.

Determine the focus

What is the problem or issue that you want to address? You might consider one of the fears that you noticed this week. Your problem would be whatever triggered the fear. Begin with the phrase “My problem is that . . .” and then state the problem in simple terms. (Pause for 15 seconds)

Identify your emotions

What do you feel when you think about your problem? (Pause) Add to your healing statement “and when I think of that, I feel . . .” Now list a few of the emotions that you have identified. (Pause for 15 seconds)

Write down your complete healing statement

Here is our example again: My problem is that I am experiencing a life change, and when I think of that, I feel lonely, distressed, anxious, and vulnerable. Be sure to write down your healing statement and keep it in a place where you can easily find it again for your KF work.

Assess the level of fear or stress

On a scale of one to ten, with ten being the most stressful for you, how stressful is your problem? Make a note of that. (Pause)

Let’s practice the first series of the Tapping Protocol together.

Sip some purified water and assume a mindfulness posture. Be sure to breathe deeply while you tap.

Opening

With the palm of each hand flat on your chest, rub in a circular motion crossing over the sternum. While rubbing, breathe deeply and repeat this statement after me:

I fully and completely accept myself as I am today. (Read 3 times, pausing for 3 seconds after each reading)

Now pause.

First Series

Continue to breathe deeply as you follow my guidance.

Using the three middle fingers of each hand, tap gently on the orbital bones under your eyes. (Repeat) Read your healing statement out loud and then breathe deeply as you tap: “My problem is . . ., and when I think of that, I feel . . .” (Pause for 15 seconds)

Now, under your dominant arm, which is most often the right arm, find a point about four inches below the armpit. Massage this rather tender spot as you repeat your healing statement. Try not to worry about finding the exact location. (Pause for 15 seconds)

Pause. With the palm of each hand facing you, push your little fingers away from you and bring your two ring fingers together so that they touch each other. Bring all the middle fingers together to form a kind of picket fence and tap on your brow, above your nose, with all six fingertips. (Repeat) Breathe, and repeat your healing statement. (Pause for 15 seconds)

Locate your collarbone and follow it out to your shoulder joints. Drop down about two inches into the crevice between your shoulders and chest muscles. Massage these two points and repeat your statement. Breathe. (Pause for 15 seconds)

Now, follow your collarbone back to the sternum. Drop down about two inches on each side of the sternum and tap or massage the space between your ribs. Repeat your statement, and breathe. (Pause for 15 seconds)

Move to the little finger of your left hand. With the three middle fingers of your right hand, tap on the inside edge of the left little finger, incorporating the edge of the fingernail. (Repeat) Repeat your statement, and breathe. (Pause for 15 seconds)

Pause. Take a deep breath and sip some water. On a scale of one to ten, how distressing is the problem or trigger event now? Jot down the number for your reference. (Pause)

This is the first series of the Tapping Protocol. In the next lesson we will learn the second series. For a much more effective tap, spend twenty seconds on each step.

Our prayer might go something like this:

1:00 BIBLE TEACHING

We will be spending some weeks in the letter from James, so you might want to print out the first chapter of James from one of the many Bible websites. Double or triple space the text to give yourself room for taking notes, and keep a copy in your ring binder with your other KF materials.

Let’s look at James 1:2.

The NASB translation reads: “Consider it all joy, my brethren, when you encounter various trials.”[6] Last week, we learned that the Greek word for consider can be translated as guide yourself. We can guide ourselves into all joy in any situation. Isn’t that amazing? However, you might wonder where this joy is that we are guiding ourselves into. Most of us have no idea where to find joy, but fortunately we do not have to know where we are going before we begin the journey. Like all journeys, we must simply begin. Jesus really is The Way, and on this journey toward reclaiming the True Self, we discover more and more about who He is and who we are becoming.[7] This is the source of all joy.

What are these various trials that James talks about? We have learned during these last weeks that trials or experiences are opportunities to discover the truth about who we are and where our real strengths and weaknesses lie. In our passage from First John, we learned that these trials are days of judgment.[8] They show us the truth about our beliefs and our fearful misorientation. Just as a courtroom will explore what is true, so this day of judgment will explore our hearts and reveal the true quality of our faith and love. Although this experience might sound intimidating, John says that we can be bold in this day of judgment. We get to see how much fear we are carrying in our souls. We also get to see how far we have travelled toward our goal of reorienting to God and living in harmony with His Creation. We no longer shrink back through fear of self-hate.[9] All of this knowing leads to our growing, and growing leads to more knowing. Through knowing and growing we gain real self-confidence. Just as the lilies grow without fear, so can you.[10]

This is what Nancy wrote about her own journey toward orientation:

In the early days of Karis Fellowships, I thought about how difficult these new ideas might be for some of you, especially if you have been trained to think in a certain way. Our KF work reminds me of how difficult it must have been for those listening to Jesus for the first time. Jesus invited them to rethink their whole culture and upbringing. They naturally felt intimidated and resistant. I thought about a time, years ago, long before we ever thought of Karis Fellowships, when I was agonizing about where Neurosis and Human Growth and my study of Paul’s letter to the Romans would take me.[11] I was afraid of my own journey toward orientation; I was afraid of going into the not-knowing. I think fear confuses all of us. When we are in fear, we feel abandoned, and in those moments, we need to be reminded that Jesus is with us.

Several years ago, while in prayer, the Lord plainly spoke to me. He said, “You must be willing to go into the not-knowing.” (Repeat) At that time, I really did not know what He meant, but all the same His words encouraged me to let go of needing to know where this journey would take me. Let me point out something. Letting go of anything is a day of judgment—and it is probably one of our more severe trials or experiments. We find out where our treasure is when we cannot let go, and that can be extremely painful. Some of us have a compulsive need to be right. I have always felt that I needed to know the right theology, the right way to do something, what is right about myself, about society, about the choices of others, and what is wrong about all of those things. I had to know what was right in order to support my pride and relieve my anxiety. During that year, God encouraged me to give up needing to know what was right. He encouraged me to just keep growing—or, shall I say, to keep going forward on the journey, producing more of the Fruit of the Spirit in my life. I knew in my heart that if I allowed the fear of being wrong to hold me back, I would be obeying my fears. I would be teaching my heart to love fear rather than to love God. What a loss that would have been! If I had followed my fears, I would not have these healing gifts to offer to my community, nor would we have Karis Fellowships.

1:10 INTEGRATION

Sip some purified water and prepare to take some notes, perhaps in a KF journal. Assume a mindfulness posture, and breathe. (Pause for 10 seconds)

Here is our integration focus: Consider going on this journey with Jesus, not knowing where you are going. (Repeat) (Pause for 30 seconds)

The FAITH Process

Feelings

As you consider going on this journey with Jesus, not knowing where you are going, how does it feel? (Pause) Your feelings are yours to teach you about yourself, to guide you, and to train you. They can often be uncomfortable. (Pause) Acknowledge your experience with compassion, whatever it may be, and breathe. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Awareness without judgment

Gently allow whatever you are experiencing to rest in your awareness. As you breathe, hold your thoughts and feelings up to the light. Observe whatever you may be experiencing as you consider going on this journey with Jesus, not knowing where you are going. (Pause) Feelings and passing thoughts are not right or wrong; they just are. Give yourself permission now to explore them without judgment, and breathe. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Inquiry: What is my body saying?

Breathe, and bring your attention to your body. Notice what it is doing when you consider going on this journey with Jesus, not knowing where you are going. What sensations are there? (Pause) Your body is always talking to you. For example, you might recognize what anxiety or resistance feels like in your body. You might feel curiosity or courage. (Pause) If your sensations are uncomfortable, you can move your attention to an area of your body that is not reacting, and rest. When you are ready, return to observe those uncomfortable sensations. Breathe. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Talk to Jesus

You might imagine that you are leaning back in the safe and loving arms of Jesus as together you consider going on this journey with Him, not knowing where you are going. Do you feel comfortable with that, uncomfortable, or are you shutting down? Just observe. (Pause) Do you judge this journey as good or evil? (Pause) What are your reasons? What story do you want to tell? (Pause) Are you afraid of something—of loss, of others, or of yourself? Observe your responses as just information and breathe with a long exhaling breath. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Heal

God created you to have all of these feelings, and for thousands of years human beings have been feeling like you do when they have gone into the not-knowing. You are not alone in your feelings. You are a human creature. (Pause) With training, you can choose what you will do with those feelings. (Pause) When a fear or judgment signals you through the body, you can focus on your body and your breath rather than getting caught up in a story and reacting. Breathe, and allow yourself to feel compassion for all human creatures who are driven by fear. Help your body process with a long exhaling breath. (Pause for 30 seconds)

(Optional) Continue to breathe while I test for further integration.

Pause. Take a deep breath.
