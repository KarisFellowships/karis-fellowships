0:08 MINDFULNESS & MEDITATION TRAINING

This is KF Confession 4:

We judged ourselves and others with contempt and felt justified in our wrath.

Here is Nancy’s account of how she took the Believe It Path when she was tempted to judge herself and others with contempt.

I had gone out of the house in an unfamiliar town without my keys or phone. When I returned, no one was at home and I was locked out. A KF meeting was due to start in thirty minutes, and I really needed to be in the house and at my computer.

I sat on the doorstep lamenting my fate and going into self-hate for forgetting phones and keys, because I should not, according to my Idol Self, forget phones and keys. Then I remembered my KF training. I used my mindfulness skills to manage my self-hate and got busy with WAA! W-A-A: Words, Attitude, Action. I used my words to pray. I changed my attitude to dismiss shame, and then I was able to creatively open my mind. I took action by boldly looking for a key hidden outside even though I knew that my friends did not leave keys outside. My first stop was the traditional front door mat and, very surprisingly, there under the mat was a key. I was able to get into the house and join the Karis Fellowships meeting on time. The mystery of this story is that my friend said that she had not left the key there. The best part of the story is that my paroxysm of self-hate and self-contempt was not allowed to muddy my prayers or my attitude. Having mercy on myself opened my mind to the possibility of taking oriented action. I was able to find the way that God in His mercy had prepared for me.

Let’s continue with the Mercy Meditation.[1]

Assume a meditation posture and breathe for one minute. Settle into being present and aware. (Pause for 1 minute)

Draw your mind away from the events of the day, from the past or the future, and from others. Focus on what is happening inside your own skin. (Pause for 30 seconds)

In this lesson, we will reflect for thirty seconds on three questions. However, when practicing this exercise on your own, you might reflect for one minute. If it works better for you, jot down your thoughts as soon as they come to mind. Sometimes our thoughts get lost if we don’t catch them on the spot.

Let’s begin our training.

1. In what areas am I most critical of myself? (Repeat) (Pause for 30 seconds)

Pause. Take thirty seconds to jot down what came to mind. (Pause for 30 seconds)

2. For what do I need to forgive myself? (Repeat) (Pause for 30 seconds)

Pause, and if you haven’t done so already, take thirty seconds to jot down your thoughts. (Pause for 30 seconds)

3. How do I treat myself when I make a mistake? (Repeat) (Pause for 30 seconds)

Pause, and if you wish, write for thirty seconds. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Now experiment with bringing compassion to yourself in some way. For example, call to mind someone who loves you just as you are. This might be someone divine, like God or Jesus, or perhaps someone mortal and human. You might call to mind one of God’s other creatures. (Pause) Imagine seeing yourself through their eyes. You might give yourself an appreciative hug. (Pause) However you choose to bring compassion to yourself, just observe and rest for one minute. Practice letting go of judgment and breathe. (Pause for 1 minute)

And pause.

Our hymn for this confession is When I Survey the Wondrous Cross.

I sacrifice them to His love.[2]

0:21 TOOLBOX

Let’s return to our Shoulds Worksheet.

We cannot give up our legal systems unless we are willing to give up our search for the Glory of Me! or, as Karen Horney puts it, the commitment to actualize the idealized self. This idealized self is an idol that we have manufactured to replace God. When we give up pursuing the Idol’s dreams, we can give up the legal systems that drive us to actualize our Idol.

Part 3 of the Shoulds Worksheet: Recovery

This section is divided into eight steps.

1. Recognize the judgment

Our first step is to look back at the Two Paths Flowchart. We have felt fear and have taken the path of judgment. We have decided that a situation is good or bad. Perhaps at this stage you are wondering: How do I take the path of love? The Shoulds Worksheet helps you learn how to do that, but don’t forget that, as a neurotic, you will be tempted to think that knowing something is the same as practicing. Let’s get real. Knowing is NOT the same as practicing. You might read a book that teaches the art of skiing, but until you go up the mountain, you will not learn to ski. You cannot learn until you are willing to fall down, get up, fall down, get up, fall down, and get up again.

Remember: Train hard. Fight easy. (Repeat)

2. Identify the fear

If you cannot perform perfectly, what do you fear? Your Idol is supported by your pride system and your search for an idealized glory. What area of your pride would be hurt if you could not perform perfectly according to your Idol’s standards? What aspect of the Idol’s dream of glory would be lost?

It hurts our pride when we cannot control other people or perfectly manage every area of our lives, when our plans don’t work out, and when an imagined future that we have judged as good is snatched away from us. Hurt pride can stop you in your tracks; you become frozen and fierce. You are then unable to move forward and be creative because you are afraid of experiencing more hurt pride. This is how pride interferes with your real potential.

3. Stick to the facts

Now that you have recognized the judgment and identified the fear, describe the situation as a simple sequence of events. This skill is the foundation of the Believe It Path. Here is an example: I went on a trip. I had plans. Someone got sick. I was not able to follow through with my plans. The End. Oh my! The Believe It Path doesn’t sound very dramatic or sensational, does it? It just is. We can’t write a soap opera or a country western song about the Believe It Path—it’s too boring! The Believe It Path gives us nothing to whisper about or to yell at others about. The Judgment Path, on the other hand, leads to conflict, and conflict is the essence of drama. Drama makes our Idol Self feel alive. Feeling alive is very important when you are not alive.

4. Make a decision: Are you willing to let go of the judgment?

When Nancy is coaching someone, she will eventually get to the question: Are you done with that? (Repeat) You have a choice. You have the power to say yes or no. When you choose to be done with your judgments, you finally give yourself permission to change.

5. Consider your progress: What have you learned?

Try to write a few sentences about what you have learned by doing this worksheet.

6. Take action: Talk to God

You have been using words to describe what the Believe It Path looks like for you. You are now in touch with the reality of your own business and can make different choices. Perhaps your attitude has changed. Now is the time to take positive action, and your first positive action is a prayer of confession.

7. Integrate

Your body may need some help orienting to the work you have done, so in this section Nancy suggests a number of different ways to help your body process your work. You can find instructions for using these methods under Toolbox on the KF website and, also, at Alison’s tapping and TAT videos.

8. Celebrate

We are all learning from each other in this training process, so we hope that you will share what you have learned with your KF community.

You don’t have to wait until after an event to do a Shoulds Worksheet. This is quite important. You can do a worksheet before you are in a situation that you know will trigger your Idol’s shoulds and self-hate. A preemptive worksheet can help you calm your body and forestall judgments and reactions. Also, by working through an upcoming situation on a worksheet, you might find it easier to stand in that situation, oriented in peace.

Our prayer might go something like this:

Lord of healing, encourage me to do the work of my own healing. (Repeat)

Take a deep breath.

1:00 BIBLE TEACHING

If you have your Bible with you, turn to the Gospel of Matthew, chapter 7.

As we have said before, legal systems do not foster gratitude. When we expect people to act in a certain way, they can do no more than meet our expectations. When they do manage to comply, we feel no real gratitude for their efforts. We only experience a brief respite from our fear of not being in control. If people only approximate our expectations, there are no points in their favor. They have failed to fulfill all the requirements of our imagined standards and, therefore, deserve our wrath. If, on the other hand, they do meet our expectations, they are not allowed to feel that they have given us a gift of any kind. They are only allowed to feel that they have escaped our wrath for a moment. Laws of shoulds and claims perpetuate contempt, ingratitude, fear, wrath, and resentment, not only toward others but toward ourselves.

Nancy studied First Corinthians 13 for many years without really understanding what Paul meant when he said, “Love does not insist on its own way, nor does it seek its own.”[3] One day, the meaning became clear to her—and it felt terrifying. She realized that if she wanted to express God’s love through her life, she would have to give up her own ideas about how things should be. She would have to submit to God’s way of doing things. Jesus taught us that if we insist on our own way, seeking the fulfillment of our shoulds, we will never experience our true glory.[4] After several years of exploration, Nancy began to learn how to exchange her personal law of shoulds and claims for oriented, healthy desires. She learned not to insist on her own way, but to meet her needs and attain her wants by asking, seeking, and knocking. This is what Jesus Himself taught us to do in Matthew 7:7–8. Let’s listen to these verses in Nancy’s translation:

7Ask, and it will be given to you; seek, and you will find; knock, and it will be opened to you. 8This is how it works: the asking one receives, the seeking one finds, and to the one who is knocking, it will be opened.

Take a deep breath.

You might like to read the whole chapter and notice how the Bible supports what we teach in Karis Fellowships. In the passage for this lesson, Jesus encourages us to stop wasting energy trying to impose our shoulds and claims; instead, He invites us to learn the process of asking, seeking, and knocking. Asking, seeking, and knocking are all qualities of a person. If you are the kind of person who keeps asking, the kind of person who keeps seeking, and the kind of person who keeps knocking on a regular basis, then Jesus is talking about you.

Asking is Words

Ask for help. Start by learning to ask questions so that you can understand the situation. Tell God and others what is going on inside your skin. Do you know that other people can’t read your mind? It is an infantile claim to expect them to do so. Toddlers learn to use their words, and so can you. Telling others what is going on inside you is not a license to impose your demands on others and on the world, nor is it an opportunity to voice your opinions about what they should or should not do to accommodate you. When you ask, you have an opportunity to practice expressing your own reality in a moderate way. Moderate expression and the ability to communicate what you would prefer is evidence that you have been doing your own work and attending to your own business.

Seeking is Attitude

When our seeking is motivated by the True Self, we are open to what we can learn about ourselves, about others, about God, and about our environment. We are not attached to an outcome that we, in our omniscient and self-righteous judgment, have deemed to be right or good. Seeking is a journey, and it opens up the possibilities of unforeseen discoveries and delightful surprises.

You might ask: If I am to seek, how do I know what to seek? You are seeking to produce the Fruit of the Spirit, which manifests through your life the attributes of love—and if you make love your destination, you will discover other very valuable things along the way, like your True Self.[5]

Knocking is Action

Explore an oriented action and see what happens. When you find a possible path, use your courage and endurance to keep knocking on the door of the not-knowing or, as Nancy did, to keep looking for the key. You really have to give up the idea that you already know what should be behind that door. With oriented effort and an open mind, you can go into the not-knowing and knock in the face of your fears. A door in your mind may open, or a door to your future may open—it’s really the same thing.

We often forget that the solutions to our problems are not a matter of just asking. We are so familiar with the acronym A-S-K for Ask, Seek, and Knock that we forget that oriented results come from a three-step process. Our Idol remembers only the easy part—“Ask and you shall receive”—so when we have asked, we think our work is done and expect God to do the rest. But remember that there’s another way to think about this three-step process: W-A-A or Words-Attitude-Action. (Repeat)

This is the time to exercise the kind of self-discipline that an excellent father would teach you. Don’t just cry “Waa!” like an immature child or act like a victim whining to the universe and blaming self, circumstances, God, and others. Instead, you can think: WAA!—Words, Attitude, Action. This process does not lead to effortless superiority. You have to work to get what you want. Use moderate words to ask so that you and others can understand what you want. Seek with an open attitude, without judgment, looking for something real that will be revealed. Knock because only action produces re-action—and if you want to make something oriented happen, you have to take oriented action. You don’t just sit around, indulge your feelings, or follow distracting rabbit trails; nor do you hold others, including God, responsible for your business. Instead, you actively look for a key under the doormat.

Our prayer might go something like this:

Holy Spirit, remind me to use Words, Attitude, Action. (Repeat)

Take a deep breath.

1:10 INTEGRATION

Sip some purified water and prepare to take some notes. Assume a mindfulness posture, and breathe. (Pause for 10 seconds)

Here is our integration focus: Consider an area of your life where you could be asking, seeking, and knocking—where you could use Words, Attitude, Action. (Repeat) (Pause for 30 seconds)

The FAITH Process

Feelings

As you ­consider an area of your life where you could use your words, seek with an open attitude, and take oriented action, how does it feel? (Pause) With compassion, recognize whatever you are experiencing, and breathe. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Awareness without judgment

Allow your experience to rest in your awareness. Observe whatever tension you might be feeling ­as you consider this area of your life where you could use your words, seek with an open attitude, and take oriented action. (Pause) Give yourself permission now to explore your thoughts and feelings without judgment, and breathe. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Inquiry: What is my body saying?

Bring your attention to your body and notice what it is doing when you consider this area of your life where you might use Words, Attitude, Action. What sensations are there? (Pause) You might notice how your body responds to the fear of hurt pride or to feelings of anxiety or resistance. (Pause) Breathe, and help your body process with a long exhaling breath or with the Dragon Breath. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Talk to Jesus

You might imagine that you are leaning back in the safe and loving arms of Jesus as together you consider this area of your life where you might use Words, Attitude, Action. Do you feel comfortable, uncomfortable, or are you shutting down? Just observe. (Pause) Are you afraid of what others might do or think, or are you afraid of something in yourself? Perhaps you fear losing your idealized dreams. Observe your responses as just information, and breathe. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Heal

As you breathe, repeat to yourself the word Welcome. (Pause for 10 seconds)

(Pause for 8 seconds or one long breath after each statement)

  • Welcome this opportunity to explore an area that might hurt your neurotic pride.
  • Welcome the Holy Spirit of truth, who is eager to train you to use Words, Attitude, Action.
  • Welcome your present reality and all the progress you have made so far.
  • Welcome what you are learning about yourself. (Pause for 10 seconds)

Now, as you breathe, you might use the following statements as a confession to the Lord and turn away from the Idol Self. On the exhale, let go.[6]

(Pause for 5 seconds after each statement)

  • As I use Words, Attitude, Action, I let go of the longing for power and control.
  • As I use Words, Attitude, Action, I let go of the longing for affection and self-admiration.
  • As I use Words, Attitude, Action, I let go of the longing for comfort and security.
  • As I use Words, Attitude, Action, I let go of the longing for perfection. (Pause for 30 seconds)

(Optional) Continue to breathe while I test for further integration.

Take a deep breath.
