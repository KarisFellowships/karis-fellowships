0:08 MINDFULNESS & MEDITATION TRAINING

Orientation springs from a state of calm, and we have been learning how to remain calm in the midst of life’s difficulties. Calming Breath meditation helps us cultivate a state of peace, which gives us the option to choose orientation.

Take a meditation posture, and breathe. (Pause) Settle into this experience and experiment. You might begin with a silent prayer. Ask God to train you and help you maintain peace within yourself and in your life. Tell Him that you want to learn to be calm in all of life’s experiments (Pause for 30 seconds)

Calming Breath

Scan for tension

Inhale and exhale for one minute, recognizing and releasing tension in your body and mind. (Pause for 1 minute)

Use sound and touch

Use the word CALM as a centering word. Sing the word, prolonging the “ahm” sound, and find a pitch that feels healing. Allow your body to soften and relax. We can train our bodies to relax immediately in response to a physical signal. So, as you continue with Calming Breath, experiment with using touch to signal your body to relax. First, on each hand, try touching together your thumb and one of your fingertips. How does that feel? (Pause for 5 seconds) Now bring together both hands, with thumbs and fingertips touching. (Pause for 5 seconds) Next, try tapping on the left side of your chest, or just lay your hand over your heart. (Pause for 5 seconds) Did any of those signals feel soothing or calming for you? Continue to experiment for one minute. (Pause for 1 minute)

Use only touch

Now, let go of singing the word and just hold the word in your mind while using your physical signal. Continue releasing tension with a long exhaling breath. (Pause for 1 minute)

Rest

Let go of the word and your physical signal. Rest in the stillness for one minute. (Pause for 1 minute)

Pause.

0:20 KARIS CONFESSION

0:21 TOOLBOX

All Creation rejoices when we commit to living in reality. Let’s continue to explore KF Confession 7:

We commit now to live in reality, choosing to believe rather than judge.

Sometimes we just have to mindfully endure suffering until its deep work is completed. Suffering is part of training hard. If you are training hard, real suffering is inevitable. However, real oriented suffering is less traumatic than the suffering that we impose upon ourselves by clinging to our pride system and trying to save our Idol’s life. If we are suffering because of hurt pride, God has to oppose us, and our suffering becomes torment. But if we choose humility, God is ready to give us gifts of grace. So, when you are suffering, give up your pride and humbly ask God for a gift that will encourage you on your way. Then watch for the gift. These special and personal gifts from God encourage you to endure the difficult process of becoming fully equipped for your joy and mission.

Think about how you might recognize healthy, productive seeds of change in your own life. These seeds will all have CORE—C-O-R-E—characteristics. C is for Courage; O is for Orientation; R is for Reality; E is for Endurance. To make the most of any challenge, you must be courageous, oriented, engaged with reality, and you must endure. You have probably heard about core muscles and the need for core strength. Without core strength, you are substantially weak; you can’t even stand up. Similarly, without CORE strength, your soul is weak and will be unable to stand in the midst of your suffering. How are you managing your challenges and your suffering? Are you acting courageously? Are you asking, seeking, and knocking for oriented solutions? Are you practicing mindful awareness with gratitude and staying in touch with what is real, or are you generating more suffering through stories, hurt pride, and self-hate? Can you endure uprightly until your oriented path is revealed? Remember CORE—Courage, Orientation, Reality, Endurance—and consider Jesus, who possessed all these core qualities and taught us how to access the redemptive potential of suffering. He exchanged His cross for joy.[2]

When we have CORE muscles, we are no longer helpless victims suffering from illusions about who we should be and the kind of life we should be living. Rather, we are explorers of what is real. We explore what God is presenting to us now for our training and development. CORE muscles give us the freedom to make oriented choices. We become the cause that sets in motion a redemptive cycle of cause and effect. Sometimes our choice results in a predictable outcome. At other times, the outcome cannot be predicted, and our motivation to make an oriented choice is based solely on our karis relationship with God. We need developed courage to go with God into the not-knowing without attaching to a particular outcome, proving to God both our trustworthiness and our trust in Him. When we choose to do things God’s way, we affirm our loyalty to Him, and we demonstrate our determination to let go of our prideful judgments about how things should be.

In your prayers this week, ask God to show you how He wants to use your present circumstances for your training. Ask Him to guide you as you make the oriented choice to keep training in those circumstances, however difficult they may be. Try working through the worksheet Suffering: An Opportunity to See Yourself. You might offer your discoveries in the sharing time next week.

KF training teaches us to calm ourselves, choose positive action, and remember how far we have come. This changes our nervous system and our emotions, which in turn changes our experience.

Here is our quote again:

Research has shown that repeated action, learning, and memory can actually change the nervous system physically, altering both synaptic strength and connections. Such changes may be brought about by cultivated change in emotion and action; they will, in turn, change subsequent experience.[3]

Our prayer might go something like this:

Lord, train my CORE muscles for courage, orientation, reality, and endurance. (Repeat)

1:00 BIBLE TEACHING

In the third chapter of his letter, James teaches us to observe our words. Your words tell you a lot about the health of your soul. What comes out of your mouth—not just your words but also your tone and delivery—is a kind of fruit, and that fruit reveals what you carry in your heart all the time. In unguarded moments or in moments of distress, self-hate, or euphoria, your words can tell you and others how your soul is really doing. However, we often do not pay attention to what comes out of our mouths. So, ask trustworthy people to reflect back to you what they notice; this is a time for humility, not self-defense. Remember to celebrate when your spontaneous words reveal the growing health of your soul and when your verbal interchanges with others indicate encouraging progress toward orientation.

In the Toolbox we talked about the CORE muscles of the soul—Courage, Orientation, Reality, Endurance. When your CORE is weak, you cannot stand in the face of compulsive behaviors, destructive habits, or sudden provocations; every part of you is vulnerable to weakness and injury. Unless you train your CORE muscles to be strong, you will be weak just when you need your strength the most.

In his letter to the Ephesians, Paul teaches us to perpetually keep our enemy in mind. If we choose to live in the lazy illusion that there is no enemy and make little or no effort to train, we will be vulnerable to enemy attack.

Let’s look at Nancy’s paraphrase of Ephesians 6:10–18 in which Paul teaches us how to prepare for the good fight against our real enemy.[4]

10And one more thing, learn to take up your abilities in the Lord and take them up in the strength that comes from doing things His way. 11Put on the complete armor of God, as when one warrior gifts his armor to another, and move toward the goal of having the ability to stand against the schemes of the slanderer and the Idol Self. 12We need this divine armor from God because our battle is not against material creatures, but against the things that rule those creatures, against the actual authorities in their lives, against the governors of their world-system that create this present darkness, and against spiritual things that cause injury in the world beyond this world. 13God has passed this battle on to you, but you cannot fight the battle for your own soul without the proper equipment. Therefore, take up the complete armor of God that He has gifted to you so that when those days of injury threaten you, you can stand against the onslaught—and doing all that you can, keep standing.

14In case you have not understood, you must stand! Wrap your core muscles with truth and cover your vital organs with orientation. 15Keep your shoes on, prepared to deliver the good news of peace at every moment. 16And in all that you do, take up the shield of trust—of believing it—with which you can extinguish all the incoming missiles that are thrown at you by those who seek to inflame you and provoke you to imagine and judge that you have been injured. 17And solemnly receive the helmet of wholeness, which protects your thoughts, declares your loyalty, and identifies you as one who has been rescued from the power of the enemy. Receive also the sword of the Spirit and the breath, which is all that God has spoken about who you are. 18 With a cultivated attitude of sacred prayer, make requests in every moment and with every breath; pray with your spirit. Always keep awake and stay alert with obstinate persistence. Keep making requests concerning the saints—I mean those who are your fellow soldiers and who, like you, have set themselves apart to serve the purposes of God.

Take a deep breath.

If you want to live an oriented life, you must accept the reality that you have an enemy. Your enemy is determined to undermine any effort you make toward restoring your True Self. When you waste your energy searching for a counterfeit glory, you are pridefully opposing God and cooperating with the enemy that seeks to sabotage God’s plan for your life, your mission, and your designed glory. Healthy fear of a real enemy motivates us to secure our boundaries and guard the ground that we have gained. It may seem like a paradox, but fear can be your friend. Fear can motivate you to undergo the difficult but necessary training that prepares you to face the enemy and gives you real self-confidence in the fight.

Paul is not saying in this passage that you can never rest. In fact, he is teaching you how to maintain a state of alert relaxation, trusting in the quality of your armor and your training. If you put on God’s armor, which is a metaphor for all the oriented qualities that you need for the fight, your enemies both within and without will no longer have power over you. Whether the enemy is Satan, your Idol Self, or the Idol Self of others is immaterial. What is important is that you acknowledge the reality that you have an enemy. Keep learning to stand when things get rough. If you rely on the armor of orientation, you will no longer need to run back to your old habits of relieving tension and restoring pride. You can just stand using your CORE muscles.

In the past, you may have judged people who are compelled by neurotic needs and self-hate, but now you are able to peacefully believe it and offer the good news of peace by being at peace. With God’s armor, you can protect yourself from the fiery missiles of others that seek to pierce your soul and ignite mindless reactions. Just stand, believing rather than judging what people choose and what people do, knowing that you do not have to participate. Spot the fools quickly and circumvent them. Recognize the wise. Walk with them and cling to them. The safest place for you to be is by their side.

Our prayer might go something like this:

Lord of the armies of God, teach me to stand. (Repeat)

Take a deep breath.

1:10 INTEGRATION

You might sip some purified water and prepare to take some notes. Assume your meditation posture, and breathe. (Pause for 10 seconds)

Here is our integration focus: Consider a distressing situation that you just can’t stand. (Repeat) (Pause for 30 seconds)

The FAITH Process

Feelings

As you consider a distressing situation that you can’t stand, recognize any emotional feelings that are present. (Pause) Our feelings are ours to teach us about ourselves, to guide us, to show us our limitations, and to train us. They can often be uncomfortable. We are learning to honor our feelings without allowing them to master us. Observe and breathe. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Awareness without judgment

Graciously allow whatever you are experiencing to rest in your awareness. Notice any struggle you may have with this experience. Do you want to escape, attack, or perhaps drift into self-pity? (Pause) Feelings and passing thoughts are part of the struggle. They are not right or wrong; they just are. Give yourself permission to explore whatever you are experiencing without judgment and use a long exhaling breath to quiet your feelings. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Inquiry: What is my body saying?

Bring your attention to your body. What is it doing when you consider a situation in which you cannot stand? For example, you might notice what shutting down, craving, or, perhaps, unfairness and rage feel like in your body. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Talk to Jesus

You might imagine that you are leaning back in the safe and loving arms of Jesus as together you consider this situation that you can’t stand. What do you believe when you consider this situation? Do you feel compelled to judge as good or evil this “reality check” about your ability to stand? (Pause) What story do you want to tell? (Pause) Observe your experience as just information, and breathe. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Heal

As you breathe, repeat the word Welcome. (Pause for 10 seconds)

(Pause for 8 seconds or one long breath after each statement)

Welcome this opportunity to explore this present distress.

Welcome the Holy Spirit of truth as He uses your suffering to heal you.

Welcome your present reality and the progress you have made.

Welcome what you are learning about yourself. (Pause for 10 seconds)

Now, as you breathe, you might use the following statements as a confession to the Lord and turn away from the Idol Self. On the exhale, let go.[5]

(Pause for 5 seconds after each statement)

As I train to stand, I let go of my longing for power and control.

As I train to stand, I let go of my longing for admiration and affection.

As I train to stand, I let go of my longing for security and comfort.

As I train to stand, I let go of my longing for perfection. (Pause for 30 seconds)

(Optional) Continue to breathe while I check for further integration.

Take a deep breath.
