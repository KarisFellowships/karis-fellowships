0:08 MINDFULNESS & MEDITATION TRAINING

I am going to read our opening prayer using Karis Fellowships vocabulary: “Examine me, O God, and reveal my motivations. Run experiments on me and expose my misoriented thinking. See if there is any hurtful or idolatrous way in me, and lead me in ways that will last forever.”

Take a deep breath.

Oriented thinking is essential for personal growth and physical well-being. Our minds and bodies are designed to function together in harmony, but we are rarely taught how to create that harmony. Although we may not be aware of it, our bodies inform our thinking, and our thinking is often related to trauma stored in our bodies. The physiological reality is that distressed bodies result in distressed minds. When the body is distressed, it sends chemical signals to the brain that cause our minds to react, and often overreact, in a simple and primitive attempt to survive.

You can calm your mind by first calming your body. That’s why, in KF, we start with mindfulness training. Daily mindfulness practice trains our bodies to calm down in those moments when we find ourselves in stressful situations that invite compulsive and addictive choices. These are the emergencies, the times when you are heating up or melting down. In these experiments, choose mindfulness, even for a few seconds, to help calm your body, open your mind to truth, and free your creativity.

We will now learn a new meditation practice taught by John Main, OSB. This Roman Catholic monk noticed that a Buddhist priest had more peace than he did, and he learned that the priest’s secret was meditation. You can read about John Main’s work and history on the web. He writes:

The all-important aim in Christian meditation is to allow God’s mysterious and silent presence within us to become more and more not only a reality, but the reality which gives meaning, shape and purpose to everything we do, to everything we are.[1]

It may take years of practice for you to experience all that he describes, but that is the nature of meditation. Meditation is “dose dependent”. The more you practice, the more you benefit.

We always begin our meditation practice by sitting upright and as still as possible. If you are ill or fatigued, you can sit propped up with pillows. Try sitting up as if you were waiting for someone or something that is just around the corner. (Pause) Breathe as you lightly close your eyes. (Pause for 10 seconds)

The Maranatha Meditation[2]

Silently, within your own mind, begin to slowly say the word MAA-RAA-NAA-THAA. Maranatha is an Aramaic word that is used as a prayer in the Bible.[3] You can let it roll on the breath like the swell of the sea, or you can simply focus on each syllable for a second or two. Remember to breathe. (Pause for 30 seconds)

As you say the word within yourself, attend to the word. Listen to it within your mind throughout the meditation time. (Pause for 10 seconds) If your mind wanders, gently draw it back to the word. We will focus on MAA-RAA-NAA-THAA for one minute. Try to let go of the sense of passing time and just relax into the word. Begin. (Pause for 1 minute)

Now pause and take a few deep breaths as you open your eyes. Gently draw your mind back to the meeting. (Pause) Through meditation, we train our mind, body, will, and heart to be still and to wait upon the Lord in any situation, no matter what is happening in us or around us.

Our hymn and prayer is Breathe on Me, Breath of God.

Breathe on me, Breath of God,

Until my heart is pure,

Until with Thee I will one will,

To do and to endure.[4]

Take a deep breath.

0:21 TOOLBOX

During these past weeks, we have been exploring KF Confession 1:

We confess that we have worshipped and served an Idol Self that we have created.

We will now move on to KF Confession 2:

We abandoned the glory that was ours, designed for us by our Creator.

In this Confession we admit that when our Idol Self is in charge, we readily abandon the glory that God designed for us.

Hopefully, you have a copy of the Two Paths Flowchart in front of you.

Training ourselves takes time, and so does healing. In this fast-paced world, we often cannot accept the limitation of time. It takes time to learn how to use KF tools, so you must think about adjusting your schedule to create space for practice and soul care. If you do not schedule time to care for yourself, you will wear yourself out in this training process. Like any craftsman who regularly uses his tools, you will learn to work faster, more efficiently, and more effectively toward your healing if you practice—and, also, if you rest.

Let’s review the KF tools that we are learning to use. These tools help us continue to make progress even when we feel discouraged, frustrated, or stuck.

KF weekly meetings and the Meeting Preparation Guide

In the Karis Fellowships meetings you are trained, healed, and reminded of your disciplines. These meetings guide your training efforts and give you a chance to share the progress and challenges that you have recorded on your Meeting Preparation Guide. Sharing your battles and discoveries with others is essential for your healing.

Our manuals—the Bible and the book Neurosis and Human Growth[5]

You may be starting to see how these two resources work together to guide you back to your design and mission.

Mindfulness and meditation practice

Mindfulness training teaches you to observe with your mind, calm your body, and exercise endurance, all of which strengthens your True Self.

Worksheets and guides—homework for lasting recovery

These worksheets and guides can be found on the KF website under Toolbox. You can start exploring them at any time. Ask your Karis Link to help you choose a worksheet or guide that might be useful in your situation. You might also attend additional training groups where you can practice using KF worksheets.

The Tapping Protocol[6]

Here is our quote from Robert Louis Stevenson: “You cannot run away from weakness; you must at some time fight it out or perish; and if that be so, why not now, and where you stand?”[7] Tapping is one way to fight it out against the weakness of your fear. After a few months of training, you will be able to quickly detect the fear, feel the strain within you, and tap.

The Two Paths Flowchart

This valuable tool reveals the path you have taken in a situation—the path of judgment or the path of agape love. If you want the Fruit of the Spirit to shine through your life, you will have to give up the judgments that fuel your Idol Self, and learn to dwell in real love.

Hopefully, you have been able to look at the Two Paths Flowchart. In our KF training, we have begun to identify our fears and the events that trigger those fears. In addition to fearful reactions, reactions of euphoria also send us down a dangerous path. Mindless euphoria is the result of judging that something is extraordinarily good. This strong emotion puts us in the Idol’s hands because we become forgetful. We lose touch with our limitations and weaknesses as creatures. We forget that we need maintenance and soul care. We forget what has happened in the past. We think that the euphoria indicates new potential, and we mindlessly take on challenges that are beyond our abilities. Then, when reality catches up with us, we are completely blindsided and fall.

The two paths of the flowchart represent the dualities that we see in the Bible—spirit and flesh, life and death, and many more. The upper path is the path of the True Self and the lower path is the path of the Idol Self. In the next few weeks, you will be introduced to a worksheet that will help you to understand and use this chart.

Look at the flowchart this week and try to notice when you are taking the lower path as you go through your day. What is the event? What assault on your Idol’s search for glory has disconnected you from God and thrown you into fear? It may help to remember that if your reaction is extreme, it is probably fear-driven. Did you judge the event as good or evil? What story are you telling about yourself, about others, or about life? What emotions are then justified by that story? What choices are you making based on those emotions? What is the outcome of your choices? Play with the flowchart this week and see what you can discover about yourself.

1:00 BIBLE TEACHING

Let’s return to the first chapter of James. Hopefully, you have printed out this chapter and have the text in front of you. We will review what we have learned in the last few meetings and then consider what agape love looks like for Christians.

Our text for this lesson is James 1:2–4. These verses have been translated by Nancy.

2Guide yourselves into all joy, my siblings, when you fall into and are surrounded by all kinds of experiments, 3knowing that these experiments examine what you trust and act upon. They also grow your ability to remain under examination in future experiments, experiments that will further explore your ever-expanding potential. 4Give this growing ability of endurance a chance to accomplish its purpose, which is to make you a mature and complete person, lacking nothing, fully equipped for your joy and mission.

Take a deep breath.

The struggle is the valuable part of our process

It is only in the struggle that we develop endurance and courage. Endurance without judgment gives us the creative space that allows us to learn how to build a glorious life and a karis relationship with God. Keep in mind that our most productive struggle is against our Idol Self—not against others or against the Creation. Our pride wants to “get it right” the first time, but we learn humility when we have to struggle to “get it right”. The humbling process of struggle disables our pride system and invites God to be our ally, whereas the easy achievement of goals often feeds our pride. Only when we have effectively dealt with our pride can we successfully handle the rewards of our achievements. Immanuel Kant, who is considered to be the father of modern philosophy,[8] taught that struggle is the indispensable accompaniment of progress.[9] (Repeat). Our popular culture has certainly strayed from that belief! Christians, however, are not bound by that culture and are invited by God to learn to struggle well. So, let’s learn how to struggle well.

The discovery and fostering of our True Self requires a lot of endurance

Any kind of growth requires a sustained effort in a consistent direction. That takes courage. For example, Karis Fellowships itself is the result of courageous, sustained effort over decades. Without the courage and endurance of several people, we would not be here. As you begin to discover your True Self, you will feel fear because you are going into the not-knowing and abandoning your search for glory. If something goes wrong, if the path isn’t easy, or if you are uncomfortable and feel that fear again, you will need courage to withstand the temptation to run back to old patterns of reacting. You will also need courage to endure your self-hate long enough to manage it productively, first by recognizing that you are in self-hate and then by using your KF tools. Your tools will help you hold an oriented course instead of escaping into compulsive addictive behaviors or imposing frantic claims on others and on life.

Let endurance have its perfect result

What is endurance? The Greek word translated as endurance means to remain under. It means to resist running away. Running away looks different for different Idol types. A mastery type runs away by self-assertion or grabbing control. A self-effacing type runs away by appeasing and complying. A resigned type just runs. It takes faith, or trust, to stand and resist running away. We learn that productive endurance is for us; it plays a crucial role in developing our True Self and revealing our real glory. Most of us were not trained to endure productively. We endured only to support the agenda of the people around us or to indulge their comfort—in other words, to pacify their Idol Self. This kind of endurance was not about developing our potential but about satisfying someone else’s compulsive needs. We suffered without any benefit to ourselves. Now, however, we have tools that make endurance beneficial and even profitable, not only for ourselves but also for this world.

Let’s switch gears a little and look at an aspect of agape love that is similar to endurance. The passage that describes Christian agape is found in First Corinthians, chapter 13. First of all, love is makrothumos.[10] That is the Greek word for this quality. Makrothumos is listed first because without it we cannot develop the other qualities of love. Makrothumos means long-tempered as opposed to short-tempered. It is sometimes translated as long-suffering. When you choose long-suffering, however, you are not choosing to just suffer for a long time. Makrothumos is strategic. It has a purpose in view and uses your discomfort to achieve that purpose.

You are learning to welcome the way that life runs all kinds of experiments on you. God uses these trials as opportunities to reveal where your trust lies, where your heart is. Ultimately, your goal is to learn to be long-tempered no matter how you feel—to refuse the easy path of giving in to your tensions; to resist running away; and, instead, to be strategic in thought, word, and action. When your True Self is connected to the Holy Spirit, you can trust God when others are panicking, snatching, controlling, or hysterical. You know what that looks like—going out of control to get control. It is fear, once again, paralyzing people and driving them to mindlessly resist, manipulate, or attack.

Do you want to live without fear? The Apostle John tells us that only love can cast out fear.[11] In Karis Fellowships, we are learning to be people of no fear who are becoming the love of God in this sorry world.

Our prayer might go something like this:

Father, help me allow Your love to cast out my fear. (Repeat)

Take a deep breath.

1:10 INTEGRATION

Sip some purified water and prepare to take some notes. Assume your mindfulness posture, and breathe. (Pause for 10 seconds)

Here is our integration focus: Consider a situation in which you cannot love yourself or someone else. (Repeat) (Pause for 30 seconds)

The FAITH Process

Feelings

As you consider a situation in which you cannot love yourself or someone else, how does it feel? (Pause) Your feelings are yours to teach you about yourself, to guide you, and to train you. They can often be uncomfortable. Recognize with compassion whatever you may be experiencing, and breathe. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Awareness without judgment

Allow whatever you are experiencing to rest in your awareness. We often flush thoughts and feelings from our awareness before they have a chance to teach us. Breathe, and hold your thoughts and feelings up to the light as you consider a situation in which you cannot love yourself or someone else. (Pause) Give yourself permission now to explore your thoughts and feelings without judgment, and breathe. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Inquiry: What is my body saying?

Breathe, and bring your attention to your body. Notice what it is doing when you consider this situation in which you cannot love yourself or someone else. What sensations are there? (Pause) For example, you might recognize what frustration or hatred feels like in your body. Breathe. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Talk to Jesus

You might imagine that you are leaning back in the safe and loving arms of Jesus as together you consider this situation in which you cannot love yourself or someone else. (Pause) Have you judged yourself or this situation as good or evil? What are your reasons? What story do you want to tell? (Pause) Is your experience a regular pattern in your life? (Pause) Are you afraid of something—of loss, of deprivation, of others, or of yourself? Observe your responses as just information, and breathe with a long exhaling breath. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Heal

God created us to have all of these feelings, and for thousands of years human beings have been feeling like you feel when you cannot love. You are not alone in your feelings. You are a human creature. (Pause) You can observe and learn something from what you have just experienced. When a fear or judgment signals you through the body, you can focus on your body and your breath rather than getting caught up in a story and reacting. Help your body process with a long exhaling breath. (Pause for 30 seconds)

(Optional) Continue to breathe while I test for further integration.

Pause and take a deep breath.
