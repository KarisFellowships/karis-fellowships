0:08 MINDFULNESS & MEDITATION TRAINING

We are exploring the Maranatha Meditation taught by John Main, OSB.[1] You can search the web for his work and history. He writes:

The all-important aim in Christian meditation is to allow God’s mysterious and silent presence within us to become more and more not only a reality, but the reality which gives meaning, shape and purpose to everything we do, to everything we are.[2]

We begin this meditation by sitting upright and as still as we can. If you are ill, fatigued, or holding a baby, sit propped up with pillows. Breathe as you lightly close your eyes. (Pause for 10 seconds)

Continue to breathe while I offer a little clarification. Maranatha is an Aramaic prayer from the Bible.[3] It can be translated three ways: Our Lord is coming! . . . Our Lord has come. . . . Come, O Lord! Silently, within your own mind, slowly say the word MAA-RAA-NAA-THAA during the meditation time. Some members find it helpful to move their lips as they attend to the word. If your mind wanders, gently draw it back to the word. We will focus on maranatha for three minutes, and I will remind you of this word at the start of each minute. Let’s begin. MAA-RAA-NAA-THAA. (Pause for 3 minutes, restating the word at the beginning of each minute)

Now pause. Take a few deep breaths and open your eyes. Gently bring your mind back to the meeting. John Main recommends that we meditate for twenty to thirty minutes twice each day.

Our hymn and prayer is Teach Me Thy Way, by Benjamin R. Ramsey.

Teach me Thy way, O Lord, teach me Thy way!

Thy guiding grace afford, teach me Thy way!

Help me to walk aright, more by faith, less by sight;

Lead me with heavenly light, teach me Thy way! [4]

Take a deep breath.

0:20 KARIS CONFESSION

0:21 TOOLBOX

This month we are considering KF Confession 2:

We abandoned the glory that was ours, designed for us by our Creator.

For this meeting, you might want to have your Two Paths Flowchart handy.

Our only hope of recovery is in Jesus. He gives us an alternative to worshipping our fears and obeying our compulsions. It is His resurrection, His example, and His love that empower us to courageously learn to be people of no fear. To follow Christ’s example, we must courageously learn to love. There is no other way to follow Him. Thankfully, we have the Bible, the Holy Spirit, and a community of fellow believers to help us heal and teach us how to love.

Let’s review some of our KF tools.

The Meeting Preparation Guide

Someone pointed out that the Meeting Preparation Guide is like a progress chart that measures how far we have come and how much we still need to grow. That’s one reason why the guide is so valuable. As you fill out the guide, you learn to tell your real story, and you may experience more peace because you are getting in touch with reality. The guide helps you celebrate your progress and admit where you need more training. Sharing what you learn about yourself is a confession to God and to the group. Genuine confession strengthens the True Self. Be sure to keep your finished guides so that one day you can look back and see how far you have come.

Our manuals

The Bible and the book Neurosis and Human Growth are our manuals in KF.[5] When you read your Bible, consider how what you are reading brings into spiritual perspective Dr. Horney's description of our battle with neurosis.

Mindfulness and meditation practice

We are now learning the Maranatha Meditation, but you also have the 3‑Minute Breathing Space in your toolbox.[6]

Worksheets and guides

Tools like the list Love or Pride? help you untangle your reactions and recognize the underlying fear.

The Tapping Protocol[7]

We introduced tapping a couple of weeks ago. If you would like to see a video demonstration, go to Alison’s tapping and TAT videos on YouTube. The Tapping Protocol script can be found on the KF website under the Toolbox tab.

The Two Paths Flowchart

This tool helps you confront the secret life of your Idol—a secret life of continual anxiety, judgments, hurt, and storytelling, all of which lead to chaotic outcomes. You can use the flowchart to trace these outcomes back to their beginnings and then to explore how you might redirect your precious energy toward healthier outcomes.

Take out your Two Paths Flowchart. I hope that you have added a printed copy to your KF ring binder.

This flowchart starts with an event. Events happen to everyone all the time. Events are those experiments that we have been talking about. Some events feel good, and some feel bad, but that doesn’t mean that the events are good or bad. It only means that we have feelings about them. Perhaps you have now learned that your feelings are untrustworthy guides for judging good and evil.

The Idol Self is driven by fear, and it reacts with judgment. When we are surrounded by various experiences and common life events that threaten our search for glory, our Idol Self reacts by blaming our circumstances or other people; it is unwilling to give up its illusions about what it deserves or how life should be. The True Self, on the other hand, recognizes that life is full of all kinds of events; it knows that, with training and with God, it is fully capable of successfully handling whatever comes. It is only through our cooperation with the love of God residing in the True Self that we can successfully navigate life’s events.

Try not to be discouraged at this point if you find yourself living on the lower path of judgment. Nancy has not met a single person who, seeing this flowchart for the first time, has said, “Isn’t it marvelous? I am consistently living on the upper path!” We live on the lower path for understandable reasons. In the coming weeks, you will learn how to begin moving from the lower path, which is the path of constant judgments and fear, to the upper path, which is the path of mindful observation and love. You will learn how to move from your sinking ship to a ship that floats.

Remember that you cannot use all of the KF tools all of the time. Schedule your own KF training time this week and take one of the following small steps toward your liberty and potential:

Read again or listen to the introduction and first chapter of Neurosis and Human Growth.[8] Listening to a book can expand our understanding of the material, and you might explore that option using a program like Audible.[9]

Review the chapters in the book that describe your neurotic solution, and you might read chapters 12–16. Begin making a short list of the kinds of events that are likely to send you down the judgment path.

Use your Meeting Preparation Guide to record events and your reactions to those events. You might review what you have written on your earlier guides and see how far you have already come.

Schedule time to read the Expanded Version of the teaching notes or email KF admin and ask for the KF Compass, which regularly delivers portions of the Expanded Version to your email.

Practice one of your tools. You might tap or, perhaps, review an event using the flowchart.

Remember, any effort toward investing in your True Self is success. Let me say that again: any effort is success. Just showing up for your meeting is success. You are not looking for perfection. You are looking for slow, small, but steady progress.

Our prayer might go something like this:

Lord, show me where to take time to practice my KF training. (Repeat)

1:00 BIBLE TEACHING

Let’s continue to explore KF Confession 2:

We abandoned the glory that was ours, designed for us by our Creator.

When you find yourself in a time of endurance, you often feel fear, which can present itself as frustration or anger. Courage is the ability to endure that fear while you make oriented choices. Your times of mindful endurance then become productive because whenever you endure your fear, you grow your courage, and this leads to more oriented outcomes. Courage is like a muscle; it is strengthened through exercising endurance in challenging situations. Developed courage is essential if you are to become a person of no fear. Here are two points that we might keep in mind.

Endurance requires long-tempered love

Endurance is not forever even though it may feel like it at times. We need endurance when we are in situations that examine our commitment, our trust, and our trustworthiness. Last week we learned about love being long-tempered rather than short-tempered. We are growing into long-tempered endurance that can resist compulsive reactions to uncomfortable and revealing events—those life experiments, those days of judgment or examination. Your KF training empowers you; you no longer find yourself without choices. Instead of giving in to the dictates of your fear, you can choose to endure your discomforts productively and respond with love and orientation.

People differ in what they must learn to endure

Always remember with compassion that people differ in what they find hard to endure. What requires dogged endurance for one Idol type may be easy for another. Our job is to help each other, but we are not called to help each other indefinitely. Some people need to find themselves without help in order to feel desperate enough to give up their idolatry and get into training. Not even God will continue to support us if we persistently hesitate and waver in our obedience to Him and consistently choose the path of our Idol Self.

Let’s return to our study of the letter from James. This is Nancy’s translation of James 1:5–8.

5But if anyone should find themselves lacking practical skill in situations, let them ask God about it. God generously gives practical help to everyone, and He gives without throwing their dysfunction back in their faces to shame and belittle them. God will give wisdom to the one who asks for skill to deal with situations, 6but that person must ask in faith. In other words, they must be ready to act upon what God shows them, without any hesitancy or wavering; for the one who wavers is like the surf of the sea, driven and tossed by the wind. 7Such persons ought not to imagine that they will receive anything from the Lord, 8being double-minded, or of two spirits, and, therefore, unstable in all their ways.

Take a deep breath.

Double-minded people cannot receive guidance from God

We see in this passage that God is willing to give to everyone the wisdom they need to become accomplished, integrated, and fully equipped. Each time you fall into a trial or an experiment, you discover areas where you need wisdom. Discovering these areas is essential for your growth. God is always willing to teach you, but you can forfeit the benefit of His guidance by being double-minded and holding open the option to judge according to your own understanding. If you find yourself continually changing your mind or choosing misorientation, it means that you are still, as yet, without conviction. Either you need more training and healing, or you need a more thorough commitment.

Every kingdom divided against itself will fall.[10] When you are acting out of neurosis, you are a divided kingdom. You vacillate between your allegiance to the Idol and your allegiance to love. You are not yet willing to take responsibility for how you perpetuate your suffering through your own choices and reactions. When your commitment wavers and is unstable, the Lord really cannot guide you to wisdom. Your house is built on sand.[11] God would love to help you, but your pride stands in His way.

We have a choice as to how we will live

When we commit to learning how to live on the upper path of the Two Paths Flowchart, our lives really do become more peaceful, more loving, and more authentic. We also discover that we have more self-control. Just as we have been trained to default to the path of judgment, we can retrain ourselves, under the guidance of the Holy Spirit, to choose the path of love. You really can do it. The Bible says you can, and empirical research says you can.

Our second Bible reading for this lesson is Proverbs 3:5–7. You might look at this passage in the context of the whole chapter, which offers encouragement and all kinds of promises to those who choose to become single-minded and learn wisdom. Here is Nancy’s version of this text:

5Make sure that all your motivation begins with trust in God. Do not lean on your own understanding. 6In all your choices be mindful of Him and He will straighten your path toward oriented outcomes. 7Do not be wise in your own estimation; fear God and turn away from doing damage.

Take a deep breath.

The quality of your relationship with God is the result of your choices. If you choose to lean on your own understanding, being wise in your own estimation and doing things your own way, you will stay on the lower path of the flowchart and disconnect yourself from God. But if you are mindful of what God values and honor His way of doing things, you will remain on the upper path and will learn to trust God with all your heart. Your greatest experiment in this world is to choose to live your life God’s way and see what happens. Fear God by obeying Him as the Boss. Let Him make your path straight. Choose today whom you will serve.[12] You really do have a choice. Trusting in God’s way of doing things is how godly choices are made.

Our prayer might go something like this:

Lord, in one area of my life this week, remind me to acknowledge You as the Boss and trust Your way of doing things. (Repeat)

Take a deep breath.

1:10 INTEGRATION

Sip some purified water and prepare to take some notes. Assume a mindfulness posture, and breathe. (Pause for 10 seconds)

Here is our integration focus: Consider a situation in which you want, so badly, to lean on your own understanding. (Repeat) (Pause for 30 seconds)

The FAITH Process

Feelings

As you consider a situation in which you want, so badly, to lean on your own understanding, how does it feel? (Pause) You can learn to recognize and honor your feelings without allowing them to master you. Recognize with compassion whatever you are experiencing, and breathe. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Awareness without judgment

Allow your experience to rest in your awareness. Breathe, and hold your experience up to the light. Observe whatever you may be experiencing as you consider a situation in which you want, so badly, to lean on your own understanding. (Pause) Feelings and passing thoughts are not right or wrong; they just are. Give yourself permission now to explore them without judgment, and breathe. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Inquiry: What is my body saying?

Breathe, and bring your attention to your body. Notice what it is doing when you consider this situation in which you want, so badly, to lean on your own understanding. What sensations are there? (Pause) Your body is always talking to you. You may not understand its language right now, but you can observe. For example, you might recognize what craving, anger, or anxiety feels like in your body. Breathe. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Talk to Jesus

You might imagine that you are leaning back in the safe and loving arms of Jesus as together you consider this situation in which you want, so badly, to lean on your own understanding. (Pause) Do you tend to judge this situation as good or evil? (Pause) Is this judgment a regular pattern in your life? What story do you want to tell? (Pause) Are you afraid of something—of loss, of hurt, of others, or of yourself? Observe your responses as just information, and breathe with a long exhaling breath. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Heal

God created us to have all of these feelings, and for thousands of years human beings have been feeling like you do when they have wanted, so badly, to lean on their own understanding. You are not alone in your feelings. You are a human creature. (Pause) You might remember that feelings are just chemical reactions in your body bringing you information. With training, you can choose what you will do with that information. (Pause) Breathe with compassion for yourself and for all those who long to lean on their own understanding. Help your body process with a long exhaling breath. (Pause 30 seconds)

(Optional) Continue to breathe while I test for further integration.

Pause. Take a deep breath.
