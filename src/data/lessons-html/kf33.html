0:08 MINDFULNESS & MEDITATION TRAINING

In this lesson we will introduce the Lectio Divina meditation. Lectio Divina, or divine reading, is a traditional Benedictine practice of meditation and prayer. Its purpose is to help us personalize Scripture and deepen our communion with God. There are four steps in the Lectio Divina. First, we read a passage from the Bible several times. We then meditate, opening our minds to what the Holy Spirit might be saying to us through the passage. Next, we respond to any impression that we receive. Finally, we contemplate, or maintain our awareness, of what we have experienced. There are no right or wrong responses to the passage. Lectio Divina is an entirely personal experience.

Lectio Divina

Take a meditation posture, with the crown of your head reaching toward the heavens and your tailbone reaching toward the earth. Allow your body to hang from the central pole of your spine. Scan your body for tension, and relax. Breathe. You might use the word CALM to center yourself for one minute. (Pause for 1 minute)

Read the passage

I will read a short text from the Bible and then pause. You may wish to focus your mind by repeating the Scripture to yourself. Observe as you allow the words to rise and fall with your breath. The text for this meditation is from John 17:1.

Glorify your Son so that your Son may also glorify You.

(Repeat) (Pause for 30 seconds)

Receive as you listen

Relax while I read this sentence three times, pausing for thirty seconds between readings. Take the attitude of a listener and observe your experience. In the stillness between the readings, allow a feeling, thought, or image to come to mind that may be related to the words. If nothing comes to mind, notice the absence of thought or just enjoy the stillness. Compassionately notice any other feelings or thoughts that may intrude, and then return to the text. Let’s begin.

Glorify Your Son so that Your Son may also glorify You.

(Read 3 times, pausing for 30 seconds after each reading)

Respond

If you were aware of an impression, an understanding, or a feeling as you listened to the words, respond to God with affirmation and gratitude. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Rest in awareness

Now rest in stillness for one minute as you hold in your awareness whatever has happened during this time. You might allow the Scripture to gently recede, and then focus your mind on your breath. (Pause for 1 minute)

Pause, and re-engage with your environment. You may wish to take this next minute to record your experience in your KF journal or on your Meeting Preparation Guide, or you may wish to rest and return to your breathing. (Pause for 1 minute)

Pause. Take a deep breath.

0:21 TOOLBOX

This month we will begin to explore KF Confession 8:

We co-labor with the Creator to seek and to save that which was lost.

Consider for a moment what people in the ancient world had to do to recover a kidnapped loved one. To achieve their goal, these people needed developed courage, resources, and skills; they needed to face realities; and they needed endurance. You are engaged in the same process, but you are not alone. The God who created you is working with you to seek and to save the person that you have lost. That person is your True Self.

Quiet self-confidence, peaceful self-management, and mindful generosity are trustworthy indicators that you have found your True Self. In KF we are looking to see progress in these areas, not perfection. Once you have experienced living in your True Self, you will want to guard the life of that self and nurture its glorious potential.

In our early KF training, we learned that there are three categories of fear. First, we might fear ourselves. We are afraid that we are crazy, worthless, or vulnerable and will fail to meet our standards, so we are harsh with ourselves and try to force a certain performance. We might also choose inertia to confine our minds and our behavior. Second, we might fear others. What others think and do terrifies us, and in order to feel safe we compulsively avoid them or try to control them. Third, we might fear loss—loss of our self-image or reputation, loss of support from others, loss of money, loss of our time or personal space, and loss of control. We might fear missing out on life.

You can probably sense right now what a huge amount of energy it takes to live in fear—energy that could be used to seek and save your True Self. If you are full of fear, it will take all the energy you have just to manage that fear and keep you functioning. Think about your fear. What are you afraid of losing—your self-value, your reputation, or missing out on the delights of this world? You may be discovering that you are more motivated by your fear of loss than by your love. (Repeat)

It takes courage to act in an oriented manner no matter how much fear we feel. You might recall our KF motto: Train hard. Fight easy.[1] (Repeat) Just as soldiers are trained to act in a disciplined manner in the face of fear, members of Karis Fellowships are trained to move through the fear into self-disciplined action. We start by training our courage. You may have read and heard that courage is the foundation of all virtue. Let me repeat that: courage is the foundation of all virtue.[2] Without courage in the face of life’s experiments, you will not take the steps that lead to real virtue, moral integrity, and principled choices. Without courage, you will find yourself retreating into old patterns of behavior, and you will justify your choices with virtue stories that restore your pride and mask your fears; and when you are faced with the challenges and enticements of this material world, the compulsion to save your life will overwhelm your best intentions.

Every time you exercise your courage and make an oriented choice in the face of fear, your courage grows. This is called training. Then, when you have to bear the heavy weight of distress or success, or you need to take the next step in your journey toward healing, you will have the courageous muscle to stand your ground no matter what your Idol Self is screaming at you. When your mission requires you to take risks, to assert your real power and influence, or to let go, you will be able to remain oriented because you have been training hard.

You might consider this: Your fear of loss may be just a shadow of the horror and grief that you unconsciously feel over a much more profound loss—the loss of your True Self. (Repeat)

Our prayer might go something like this:

Father, for Your sake, I want to give up my search for glory and seek my True Self. (Repeat)

1:00 BIBLE TEACHING

In the second chapter of his letter, James writes about faith. To give us a better understanding of what James is teaching us, Nancy has introduced other accurate and valid translations of the Greek word for faith, including trust, believe, and a set of beliefs.

Let’s listen now to Nancy’s paraphrase of James 2:10–23.

10If you try to use the law of your Idol’s system and then fail in one area of your law, you will condemn everything about yourself. 11The same Lawgiver who says, “Do not be sexually untrustworthy,” also says, “Do not be murderous.” In other words, if you choose to live by law and be chaste, but are still murderous, you make yourself fail the whole law. 12Instead, speak and also act like citizens who are brought to court by the law of freedom rather than like slaves who are brought to court by the law of the Idol. 13The resulting verdict of the court according to the Idol’s law will be inexorable for those who are inexorable for they cannot help but punish themselves and others through self-hate. Mercy, however, exults and is self-confident in trials. Mismanaged self-hate throws us back into slavery, but having mercy on ourselves and on others is the new way to handle mistakes and invite possibilities. Mercy is real self-confidence in action.

14How does it benefit you, or anyone, if you say that you believe but do not act on what you believe? Your belief will not rescue you or anyone else. 15And that lack of congruence between your beliefs and actions will be exposed when you encounter a member in the community who is in need. 16For example, you say to someone in need, “Go away from me in peace with my blessing of warmth and food,” but you do nothing practical about their need. Their need is stirring up conflicts in you, and since you won’t deal with your own inner conflict about showing mercy to yourself, you refuse to deal with any conflict about mercy. 17In this way, relying on a set of beliefs without taking action destroys your faith’s vitality. 18Look at it this way. You say that you trust God, or have a set of beliefs that you trust, but I take action. Show me your trust apart from action, and I will show you my trust by my action. Which is more real? 19You say that you believe that God is undivided in His person and in His activities, and that is admirable, but even demons believe that. Unlike you, however, who separate faith from action, demons are congruently afraid of God. 20You vain and pretentious people, are you willing to recognize that your faith separated from your action is not doing its job? It is not preparing your soil for seeds of orientation. 21Abraham, who was our first example of a person living in a relationship with God based on faith, was oriented through his action—the action of taking Isaac, his son, up to the place of sacrifice. In the same way, you are learning to sacrifice your dreams of an idealized glory. 22You can see that his action was congruent with his faith, and through his action his faith reached its fulfillment. That fulfillment was orientation to God. Abraham’s gift to God in karis relationship was his effort, not his set of beliefs. 23And what is written was fulfilled: “Abraham was trustworthy with God, responding to God with his karis gift, and this act—this offering—was credited to Abraham as orientation, and he was called the beloved friend of God.”

Take a deep breath.

Let’s look at a few points that James presents in this passage.

The law cannot rescue you

You cannot rescue the True Self by imposing legal systems on your life. It is impossible to fulfill all the requirements of a legal system, and if you try, you will always be in danger of self-condemnation.

Freedom is what kidnapped slaves are seeking

Slaves have no power over their own lives, and yet they can envision a life of freedom. In Christ, we are no longer slaves of the Idol but citizens living under the law of liberty, and as citizens we have the freedom, the resources and, also, the responsibility to change our lives.

Mercy is self-confidence in action

Mercy triumphs over judgment.[3] Mercy is the law of Jesus in the court of freedom and is the opposite of self-hate. Under the kingdom law of karis, there is no condemnation, so we are free to make mistakes, find out what works, and then become the cause of our own beneficial outcomes, which builds more self-confidence.

Mercy toward others

The unseen mercy that we show to ourselves is manifested in the material world by the mercy we show to others. Mercy is an active, effortful self-extension that shows God we mean business about His mission, His priorities, and our relationship with Him. Showing mercy to our community is often difficult. It requires courage, and it costs us something—and that’s why mercy is a karis gift.

God is united in effort and principle

We are called to be imitators of God, acting in congruence with what we know to be good, right, and fair.[4] James reminds us that what God says, He does. He doesn’t compartmentalize, believing one thing and yet doing something else.

Abraham gave the karis gift of obedience

In obedient response to God’s request, Abraham relinquished what he loved most—his son, who was his hope for the fulfillment of his search for glory. Abraham was congruent. He acted on what he believed about God, and through his choices, not through his ideas or good intentions, he became God’s beloved friend.

Our prayer might go something like this:

God of Abraham, help me become Your trustworthy friend. (Repeat)

Take a deep breath.

1:10 INTEGRATION

Sip some purified water and prepare to take some notes. Assume a meditation posture, and breathe. (Pause for 10 seconds)

Here is our integration focus: Consider becoming God’s trustworthy friend. (Repeat) (Pause for 30 seconds)

The FAITH Process

Feelings

As you consider becoming God’s trustworthy friend, how does it feel? (Pause) Your feelings are yours to teach you about yourself, to guide you, and to train you. They can often be uncomfortable. (Pause) Recognize with compassion whatever you may be experiencing, and breathe. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Awareness without judgment

Graciously allow your experience to rest in your awareness. Notice if there is any tension or resistance as you consider becoming God’s trustworthy friend. (Pause) Feelings and passing thoughts are not right or wrong; they just are. Give yourself permission now to explore them without judgment, and breathe. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Inquiry: What is my body saying?

Bring your attention to your body and notice what it is doing as you consider becoming God’s trustworthy friend. What sensations are there? (Pause) For example, you might notice what hope, pessimism, or shame feels like in your body. You might feel nothing at all. Gently maintain awareness of whatever you are experiencing, and breathe. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Talk to Jesus

You might imagine that you are leaning back in the safe and loving arms of Jesus as together you consider the possibility of becoming God’s trustworthy friend. Is your experience right now comfortable, uncomfortable, or vague and slippery? Just observe. (Pause) What do you believe when you consider becoming God’s trustworthy friend? What story do you want to tell? (Pause) Are you, perhaps like Abraham, afraid of loss? Observe your experience as just information, and breathe. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Heal

As you breathe, repeat the word Welcome. (Pause for 10 seconds)

(Pause for 8 seconds or one long breath after each statement)

Welcome this opportunity to explore friendship with God.

Welcome the Holy Spirit of truth as He reveals the realities of your faith.

Welcome your present reality and the progress you have made.

Welcome what you are learning about yourself. (Pause for 10 seconds)

Now, as you breathe, you might use the following statements as a confession to the Lord and turn away from the Idol Self. On the exhale, let go.[5]

(Pause for 5 seconds after each statement)

As I learn to be God’s trustworthy friend, I let go of the craving for power and control.

As I learn to be God’s trustworthy friend, I let go of the craving for admiration and affection.

As I learn to be God’s trustworthy friend, I let go of the craving for security and comfort.

As I learn to be God’s trustworthy friend, I let go of the craving for perfection. (Pause for 30 seconds)

(Optional) Continue to breathe while I check for further integration.

Pause. Take a deep breath.
