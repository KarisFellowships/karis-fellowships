0:08 MINDFULNESS & MEDITATION TRAINING

KF Confession 11 presents one of our more difficult challenges as we heal:

We take responsibility for any damage that we have done.

All of us have inherited debts of misorientation. The Bible teaches that the misorientation of our ancestors and its effects have been passed on to us, and we have also been damaged by the misorientation of those around us.[1] We, too, have generated misorientation—we have damaged the material world, our communities, and our own lives. Let’s stop pretending that this isn’t so and get on with the glorious work of redeeming our debts. The spiritual discipline of meditation strengthens our ability to endure as we take responsibility for the damage we have caused.

The Sunlight Meditation

At this point in our training, we have been introduced to a variety of meditation techniques. As we explore this next confession, we will be reviewing some of those techniques with this added emphasis: we will focus our minds on our foreheads and lift our eyes during the meditation.

Assume a meditation posture and gently close your eyes. Allow your body to regulate your breathing as you begin Alternate Nostril Breathing. (Pause for 2 minutes)

Pause and relax. (Pause) Now, with your eyes closed, imagine that you are sitting outside on a sunny day. Gently raise your eyes toward the sun without lifting your chin. You might feel a tiny stretch. Imagine warmth and light shining down on your forehead. Try to maintain that sensation throughout this meditation practice. As you breathe, you can use the Maranatha Meditation to help you focus. We will practice for three minutes. Let’s begin. (Pause for 3 minutes)

Pause. Take a deep breath and stretch. Gently open your eyes and re-engage with the meeting.

0:21 TOOLBOX

Here is KF Confession 11 once again:

We take responsibility for any damage that we have done.

In this confession, Nancy has used the word damage, which can also be understood in terms of debt. Restoring damage is like paying off a debt. It often feels tedious and burdensome because we have to expend resources that could be used for pursuing our idealized glory and maintaining the illusion that we can live free from limitations. We have all created debt through our own choices, and it hurts our pride to admit that life and God won’t magically cancel those debts for us. Taking responsibility for our debts requires “a long obedience” to a guiding principle, and this obedience is not a wasted effort; we are trained through practicing a long obedience.[3]

When we do the MATH, the final resource to consider is our health. Each human being is a matrix of soul, mind, emotions, and material body. If we neglect the limitations of that design, we will go into health debt. Waking up to the consequences of this neglect has brought many of us to Karis Fellowships.

Nancy includes your health under KF Confession 11 because she believes that all of us have damaged our health in some way. God is inviting you to come to Him with the reality of your health and the real limitations that it presents. Without health—whether physical, mental, emotional, or spiritual—you cannot live a glorious life, but if you co-labor with God as your Guide and Physician, you can seek and rescue that which was lost. Your mind and soul have the capacity to comprehend the glory and love of God, and your body is the vehicle through which that glory and love are revealed. You are the evidence of the unseen reality of your Creator, and for His sake you need to restore your health. To do that you will need help. Getting help requires spending other resources in the MATH. For example, you need money to hire help, and you must allocate time to develop the abilities and habits to care for the health of your body, mind, and soul. It’s never too late to improve your health.

People experience compromised health for a number of reasons. We may, for example, have been born with poor health, or we may have been damaged by adverse circumstances. That is all part of living in a messed-up world. However, we can improve compromised health for the glory of God. In fact, health limitations may be the resistance you need to build the strong CORE muscles of a trustworthy character.[4] Perhaps, one day, God might step into the material world and heal you, but in the meantime, as you live within the limitations of the health that is yours today, you can show Him that you are trustworthy.

Take responsibility for your physical health and well-being. You might visit alternative practitioners who can help you heal naturally and avoid future health problems. Illness often robs us of the money, ability, and time that we need to find and explore solutions, so explore your options before you are in a health crisis and running scared. Then, when you or someone you love gets sick, which is a certainty in this world, you will know where to go for help.

The struggle to find your healing path strengthens your CORE—your courage, your orientation, your commitment to reality, and your endurance. Before you find your path, you will have to expend some of your resources exploring your possibilities. Losses in the process are natural. They are part of your struggle and part of your training. With oriented eyes, learn to see loss as a natural result of living within limitations. Limitations narrow your playing-field, focus your efforts, and train you in efficiency. Value these limitations for their part in helping you learn to ask, simplify, adventure, and set priorities.

Spend time with people who are recovering their health. Ask, seek, and knock. Talk to those who are already involved in recovery. Just as you would seek out a skilled doctor or plumber, you will need to look around for a skilled alternative practitioner or therapist. Alternative healers can often be found through the referral network of health-food stores, gyms, and yoga studios. Search for them on the web, but be sure to examine their reputation and skill set before you decide to pay for their services. You might ask KF members to recommend practitioners and therapies.

Our prayer might go something like this:

Jesus, open my eyes so that I might follow You on the path to optimal health. (Repeat)

Take a deep breath.

1:00 BIBLE TEACHING

In this lesson, we will learn from a woman who unsuccessfully pursued her healing for twelve years. She spent her limited resources of money, ability, and time—in essence her life—going to doctors, but she could not find a cure. Some of us know what she went through. Nancy has spent over forty years pursuing her healing. During that time, she has experienced miraculous healing, and she has also found substantial healing through conventional and alternative medicine. Through enduring that long struggle, Nancy gained the self-confidence that she has today. She learned to appreciate her efforts and clarify her priorities. She also learned where her character lacked a strong CORE. Without those years of struggle, Karis Fellowships would not exist.

This is Nancy’s translation of Luke 8:43–48.

43And there was a woman in the crowd who had been hemorrhaging for twelve years. She spent her life going to doctors and none of them were able to heal her. 44She came up behind Jesus and touched the fringe of His robe. Immediately, the bleeding stopped. 45“Who touched me?” Jesus asked. Everyone denied it, and Peter said, “Master, this whole crowd is pressing up against you.” 46But Jesus told him, “Someone touched me, for I felt healing power go out from me.” 47When the woman saw that her actions were not hidden, she began to tremble and fell to her knees before Jesus. The whole crowd heard her declare that she was the one who had touched Him and that she had been immediately healed. 48“Daughter,” He said to her, “your trust has made you whole. Go forward in peace.”

Take a deep breath.

In this passage, we might notice how the sick woman took responsibility for her physical damage, or debt, and through that process found Jesus. She was an unusual woman living outside the common roles of her society. Like many members of Karis Fellowships, she was driven by desperate need to abandon the conventions of her culture and pursue the healing that only comes from a relationship with Jesus.

  1. She had already put lots of effort into her healing

Before Jesus healed her, the woman had already made her physical healing a priority, and that priority was factored into all of her life decisions.

  1. She was very assertive about the importance of her health

This suffering woman was no longer a passive recipient of the ministrations of doctors. She believed that her health was her responsibility, and she did not let pride, conventions, social roles, or her fears stand in her way as she pursued health.

  1. She had learned courage and endurance while making oriented choices in the real world

Her ill-health was a reality. The long years of living with debilitating health had taught her courage, orientation, how to grapple with reality, and endurance. When she saw her opportunity in Jesus, she used her CORE muscles to grab Him.

  1. She had learned to be honest and speak her reality with self-confidence

It’s scary to touch the unseen and get instantly healed. Another world opens up to you, like the ability to fly when you have unwittingly fallen out of the nest. Through the long years of struggle, this woman had trained herself to act in the moment according to her beliefs, despite her fear. She was able to honestly answer Jesus with humility and truth without worrying about what others might think or how they might judge.

  1. Jesus said that her faith, or her action based on her trust, had saved her

The woman’s choices in the material world were the evidence of what she believed in her heart. This partnership of ideas and action made her whole. The Greek word for whole is commonly translated as saved. Wholeness comes when we live congruently as the children of God—when our choices reflect our oriented beliefs. We are then unified and complete. We are glorious.

  1. Her choices made her healing happen

Perhaps the most important lesson that we can learn from this story is that the woman’s will, and not the will of Jesus, makes her healing happen. She is healed after her character has developed and her desires have become clear and focused. Through her long struggle, she has acquired a taste for action, so when her opportunity comes, she is ready to act. She reaches out to Jesus based on her trust in Him. Jesus commends this congruence of belief and action, which brings her peace. Her internal conflict is finally resolved. She is saved, unified, and complete, and her physical health in the seen world now reflects the unseen reality of her soul.

Make your health a priority. Restore your own spiritual, physical, mental, and emotional health so that you can journey forward in peace. No one can do it for you. You must take action and learn how to touch Jesus for yourself.

Our prayer might go something like this:

Jesus, help me train today so that one day I might touch You and become whole. (Repeat)

Take a deep breath.

1:10 INTEGRATION

Sip some purified water and, if you wish, prepare to take some notes. Assume your meditation posture, and breathe. (Pause for 10 seconds)

Here is our integration focus: Consider an area of health—whether spiritual, physical, mental, or emotional—that continues to invite restoration. (Repeat) (Pause for 30 seconds)

The FAITH Process

Feelings

As you consider an area of health—whether spiritual, physical, mental, or emotional—that continues to invite restoration, how does it feel? (Pause) Your feelings are yours to teach you about yourself, to guide you, and to train you. They can often be uncomfortable. (Pause) Recognize with compassion whatever you are experiencing, and breathe. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Awareness without judgment

Allow your experience to rest in your awareness as you consider this area of limited health. You might notice a compulsion to make up a virtue story or to push away thoughts and feelings. We often flush away thoughts and feelings before they have a chance to teach us. Observe whatever tension you might be feeling, and breathe. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Inquiry: What is my body saying?

Bring your attention to your body and notice what it is doing when you consider this area of health that continues to invite restoration. What sensations are there? (Pause) Your body is always talking to you. You may not understand its language right now, but you can observe. For example, you might recognize what denial or hopelessness feels like in your body. You might feel inspired. Breathe. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Talk to Jesus

You might imagine that you are leaning back in the safe and loving arms of Jesus as together you consider this area of health—whether spiritual, physical, mental, or emotional—that continues to invite restoration. Do you judge the need for restoration as good or evil? (Pause) What do you believe when you think about this area? What story do you want to tell? (Pause) Are you afraid of something? Observe your responses as just information, and breathe with a long exhaling breath. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Heal

As you breathe, repeat the word Welcome. (Pause for 10 seconds)

(Pause for 8 seconds or one long breath after each statement)

  • Welcome this opportunity to explore your health.
  • Welcome the gentle Holy Spirit of truth as He seeks to heal and make you whole.
  • Welcome the present reality of your health and the progress you have made.
  • Welcome what you are learning about yourself. (Pause for 10 seconds)

Now, as you breathe, you might use the following statements as a confession to the Lord and turn away from the Idol Self. On the exhale, let go.[5]

(Pause for 5 seconds after each statement)

  • As I begin to honor my health, I let go of the longing to have things go my way.
  • As I begin to honor my health, I let go of the longing for self-satisfaction and admiration.
  • As I begin to honor my health, I let go of the longing for security and comfort.
  • As I begin to honor my health, I let go of the longing for perfection. (Pause for 30 seconds)

(Optional) Continue to breathe while I test for further integration.

Pause. Take a deep breath.
