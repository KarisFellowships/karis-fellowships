0:08 MINDFULNESS & MEDITATION TRAINING

This month we are looking at KF Confession 3:

We constructed legal systems based on our own assessment of good and evil.

In recent lessons we have been using mindfulness training to explore our legal systems. We create legal systems because we are afraid of something. We think that these laws will help us feel safe in a chaotic world. Through the SALT Process, you are learning to examine your legal systems by identifying an area of your life that you have judged as evil. You are also learning to look at the fear behind your judgment.

Training is always somewhat difficult because you have to tear down what you have previously built so that you can build something new. If you struggle with the mindfulness training, observe the struggle without judgment and simply allow it to be. You are building new brain pathways, and that is hard work. Your endurance gives those new brain paths time to grow. It takes a great deal of effort to forge a new path through the jungle of your mind, so think of yourself as an explorer carving out a new path toward mindfulness. Later in your training, you might increase your meditation time to twenty minutes, twice each day. You are creating a new habitat where you will be able to remain peaceful, observe without judgment, know what is your business, and have a real choice as to how you will speak and act.

Let’s continue practicing the SALT Process.

  1. S is for Skin

Assume a mindfulness posture. (Pause) Focus your mind on your body, and breathe. Feel the air move in and out. (Pause) Notice where your skin is. Perhaps you can feel the air or clothing on your skin. Notice how your skin provides a delineation between what is outside and what is inside. Your skin is a physical boundary. (Pause) Now, guide your attention to observe only what is within your skin, and breathe. (Pause for 30 seconds)

  1. A is for Assess

Allow an event or situation to come to mind, one that you have judged as evil. Observe how your body feels when you think of that event or situation. (Pause) Is there tension, fatigue, restlessness, or something you cannot describe? Where in your body do you feel those sensations? (Pause) On a scale of one to ten, assess the intensity of what you are feeling. (Pause) If you feel discomfort that is too distracting for you, use the Dragon Breath to dissipate the tension, breathing loudly and powerfully from your diaphragm. Breathe. (Pause for 30 seconds)

  1. L is for Look

Look for the fear. Ask yourself and ask God: What frightens my Idol Self about this event or situation? What seems to undermine my search for glory? (Pause) Step back from judging the event and simply look at the fear behind the judgment. Observing the fear brings you valuable information about yourself and is part of your healing process. (Pause) You might try welcoming the fear and thanking it for what it is telling you about the quality of your faith, or your trust in God. Thank the fear for its part in motivating you to grow. As you thank the fear, use a long exhaling breath, like a sigh. (Pause for 30 seconds)

  1. T is for Tell

Tell God about the fear. Talk to Him about what frightens you and about the stories that you use to justify your fear. (Pause) Are your stories about the past or the future, about others, or about yourself? (Pause) Ask God to tell you the truth about this situation that frightens you and to replace the fear with the positive motivations of hope, trust, and love. Offer Him gratitude. Breathe. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Pause, and take a few relaxing breaths. You may wish to take a sip of water and jot down a few notes about what you have learned, or you might simply rest in the stillness for one minute. (Pause for 1 minute)

As you rest, listen to our hymn for this confession, All the Way my Savior Leads Me.

All the way my Savior leads me, cheers each winding path I tread,

Gives me grace for every trial, feeds me with the living Bread.

Though my weary steps may falter, and my soul athirst may be,

Gushing from the Rock before me—Lo! A spring of joy I see;

Gushing from the Rock before me—Lo! A spring of joy I see.[1]

Take a deep breath.

0:21 TOOLBOX

This month we are exploring KF Confession 3:

We constructed legal systems based on our own assessment of good and evil.

The Two Paths Flowchart begins with an event. Right now we are concerned with events that trigger a fear reaction. If we perceive the event as a danger or a threat to our Idol Self, we judge it as evil. If the event seems to deliver us from danger, we judge it as good. The judgment, however, more than the event, is what hurts us. (Repeat)

We are so afraid of messing up, of others messing up, or of life messing up according to our ideas of good or evil, and yet these feelings of fear are not evil in themselves—the fear of lost opportunity and potential may actually motivate us to do the work of recovery. You can learn to recognize fear in your body whenever you feel strain, the need to force your reality, the craving to run away, the urge to appease, or the need to control, undermine, attack, or play the victim. Even if you can’t name your fear, you can take the Believe It Path instead of reacting on autopilot like a silly rabbit that runs into the hunter’s snare. As you work through the Two Paths Worksheet, you are training yourself to ask: Do I want to take the Judgment Path or the Believe It Path? Just by asking yourself that question, you choose to disengage from autopilot and refuse to be unwittingly driven into slavery by your fear; you pause and consciously choose to endure the tension of saying no to fear while you train to achieve a healthier outcome.

Unexamined fear tricks you into taking the Judgment Path over and over again. In KF, you are learning to recognize misoriented fear for what it is—simply a habitual physical reaction triggered by unprocessed trauma. Exchange your fear for wonder. Practice using the energy devoted to your fear to develop your faith, or your trust, in God and in His design. As you develop your trust, you will become less and less fearful and spend less of your life on autopilot.

Your faith or trust is your business, and learning to trust is your responsibility. You cannot depend on anyone else to learn and exercise faith for you. However, you will need training. Training means that you submit to a program for a time until you can learn to productively direct yourself. Training gives you vision for what is possible, and it helps you develop the skills and resources required to achieve that vision.

The Idol Self would have you believe that you can tackle all kinds of situations, relationships, or work, without any training or effort. It even tells you that God makes things happen for you without your participation. Like magic, you should be able to manage every situation heroically. It’s crazy! The Idol cannot tolerate being a mere mortal in need of training. Everyone needs training in everything, and Karis Fellowships is one of the many programs available to help you train.

Your business is to get training, but the Idol Self is against you doing your business, so you may actively distract yourself from that pursuit. Working on your business may feel loathsome for a little while, and that’s why the effort you make to deal with your business is another way of losing your life for Christ’s sake.[2] You can learn to submit to the limitation that your business has to be done in this world and can only be done by you. That worthwhile and rewarding process requires endurance and cooperation with God’s training as He works to bring you into your real glory and potential.

The Two Paths Worksheet trains you to take the path of love, which leads to oriented outcomes. It teaches you how to untangle the jumble of judgments, thoughts, and feelings that are often triggered by life’s events and people’s choices. When you practice the Believe it Path, you learn not only to recognize your business but to manage your business and to stop wasting your energy on unproductive judging.

Next week, we will look at the Shoulds Worksheet. Print out a copy for your KF ring binder and explore it this week, perhaps with your Karis Link.

Our prayer might go something like this:

Lord, help me take up the work of my business. (Repeat)

Take a deep breath.

1:00 BIBLE TEACHING

Let’s look again at the first chapter of James.

In our last lesson, we learned from James that the deceitful gifts of the hunter harm us, while God’s gifts rebirth us into our design and potential. If we are to courageously reject the hunter’s gifts, we must have vision for what God’s gifts can do. Hopefully, through KF, you are catching a glimpse of that vision.

Let’s read Nancy’s translation of James 1:18.

18God never intended death to be the harvest of these times of trial; rather, it is His intention to produce our True Selves by the creative process and the first principle of truth so that we would be a kind of firstfruits among His creatures.

What does it mean to be firstfruits? It means that we, in Christ, have become something completely new. We are a new family expressing the Person and character of God, our Father, in a way that the world has not yet experienced.[3] Later in this chapter, James teaches us that in order to become those firstfruits, we have to take responsibility for our business. Let’s take a minute to expand our understanding of the Greek word logos, which we touched on in the KF Introductory Meeting. Logos can be translated as word, but it can also be accurately understood as God’s way of doing things. Jesus was the Logos; He showed us how to do things God’s way. It is our business to get rid of all those things that interfere with our ability both to receive and to bind ourselves to the logos.

Here is Nancy’s paraphrase of James 1:21–25.

21Therefore, wiping away all filthiness and all that remains of cowardice, in humility receive the logos implanted, which is able to rescue your True Self. 22Become doers of the logos and not just hearers who will not evaluate themselves accurately; 23because if anyone is a hearer of the logos and not a doer, that person is only an image, an illusion, and is revealed to be the kind of person who only glances at the logos as they would glance into a mirror simply to see their natural face. 24How do we know that? Because once that illusion-person has compared themselves to the logos and gone away, they immediately forget what kind of person they actually are. 25But the one who makes an effort to lean on the logos, which is the fulfilling law of real freedom, and works to remain near it—not having become a forgetful hearer, but an effortful doer—that person will be happy and favored in all of their doing.

Take a deep breath.

Earlier in this chapter, James taught us that we are lured by the hunter, or get into the wrong pasture, because of our cravings and greed. We learned that the outcome of those cravings is death. James contrasts the outcome of death with the creative principle of God’s way of doing things—the logos—which leads to life. One of the many things we can see in this passage is the need to get rid of all the stuff that is clouding, muddying, and interfering with an honest self-evaluation. We need to clean the mirror. By doing so, we get into the right pasture and are then able to humbly accept the logos, God’s way of doing things. When we embrace God’s way, our True Self is rescued, and we are granted a place of favor in our karis relationship with Him.

Our business is to practice what we learn and become doers of the logos, but, like all learners, we will make mistakes and be inconsistent. We can’t do it all, all of the time, and right now. So, as you explore your KF tools, find the ones that work for you. You are presently collecting tools and exploring how helpful they might be. Choose a tool and use it for a few weeks, and then set it aside while you practice another one. Return to the tools that work for you.

What is this law of freedom? How does James describe it? The law of freedom is the logos, and the logos is described as telos, which is often translated as perfect. Telos is the fulfillment of a designed purpose. Your designed purpose is to grow in the logos and learn to live in this law of real freedom. If you are in that process, you are fulfilling the logos and are, therefore, perfect or telos. Thomas Sterner in his book The Practicing Mind offers a metaphor that describes the perfection of a growing thing that fulfills its created design at every stage of its development. He writes:

As we attempt to understand ourselves and our struggles with life's endeavors, we may find peace in the observation of a flower. Ask yourself: At what point in a flower's life, from seed to full bloom, does it reach perfection?[4] (Repeat) (Pause)

Our prayer might go something like this:

Lord of the law of freedom, help me be a perfect doer of the logos at whatever stage I am. (Repeat)

Take a deep breath.

1:10 INTEGRATION

Sip some purified water and prepare to take some notes. Assume a meditation posture, and breathe. (Pause for 10 seconds)

Here is our integration focus: Consider an area of your life where you might choose to do things God’s way. (Repeat) (Pause for 30 seconds)

The FAITH Process

Feelings

As you consider an area of your life where you might choose to do things God’s way, how does it feel? (Pause) Your feelings are yours to teach you about yourself, to guide you, and to train you. They can often be uncomfortable. (Pause) Recognize with compassion whatever you may be experiencing, and breathe. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Awareness without judgment

Allow whatever you are experiencing to rest in your awareness. You might notice a compulsion to push thoughts or feelings away, to judge them, or to justify them. Breathe, and hold your experience up to the light as you consider an area of your life where you might choose to do things God’s way. (Pause) Feelings and passing thoughts are not right or wrong; they just are. Give yourself permission now to explore them without judgment, and breathe. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Inquiry: What is my body saying?

Breathe, and bring your attention to your body. Notice what it is doing as you consider this area of your life where you might choose to do things God’s way. What sensations are there? (Pause) For example, you might recognize what resistance or the fear of losing control feels like in your body, or perhaps what courage or trustworthiness feels like. Breathe. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Talk to Jesus

You might imagine that you are leaning back in the safe and loving arms of Jesus as together you consider this area of your life where you might choose to do things God’s way. Are you comfortable, uncomfortable, or are you shutting down? Just observe. (Pause) Do you judge God’s way as good or scary? What are your reasons? What story do you want to tell? (Pause) Is this experience a regular pattern in your life? Are you afraid of something—of loss, of others, of deprivation—or perhaps you are afraid of your true glory? (Pause) Observe your responses as just information, and breathe with a long exhaling breath. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Heal

God created you to have all of these feelings, and for thousands of years human beings have been feeling like you do when they have considered choosing to do things God’s way. You are a human creature and are not alone in your feelings. (Pause) When a fear or judgment signals you through the body, you can focus on your body and your breath rather than getting caught up in a story and reacting. Breathe with compassion for yourself and for everyone who is making the effort to live by the logos. Help your body process with a long exhaling breath. (Pause for 30 seconds)

(Optional) Continue to breathe while I test for further integration.

Take a deep breath.
