0:08 MINDFULNESS & MEDITATION TRAINING

In this lesson we will practice pacing the Three-Part Breath while relaxing the perineum and the face muscles. This technique strengthens our ability to focus our minds and manage our bodies. It may also generate a little stress, which gives us the opportunity to exercise courage and endurance. Spend a few minutes practicing this breathing method at the start of your own meditation time. Your goal is to maintain a connection between your mind and body while disconnecting from your stories and distractions. When you find your mind wandering, kindly say to yourself, “Release thoughts,” and then gently reconnect to your breath. Let’s start with relaxed, natural breathing. (Pause for 30 seconds)

The upright meditation posture gives your body more room to expand. Sit up straight with the crown of your head reaching toward the heavens and your tailbone reaching toward the earth. Lower your chin a little as you lengthen the back of your neck. Breathe naturally while maintaining this posture. (Pause) Observe how your body moves and how your back expands as you breathe. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Let’s begin the Three-Part Breath. You don’t need to work hard at this. Try not to create tension in your body by forcing air either in or out. (Pause for 10 seconds)

Relax your throat and face. It might help to maintain a little Mona Lisa smile throughout the entire breath. Open the space between your eyebrows by allowing the muscles on both sides of your face to relax. Release any tension between your eyebrows and in your jaw. (Pause for 30 seconds)

When exhaling, focus on the expanded lower ribs and abdomen while the upper chest relaxes. Then allow the lower ribs to deflate just before the abdomen begins collapsing. As you gently pull your abdomen toward your spine on the exhale, keep your perineum and pelvic area relaxed. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Now we will try pacing the breath. During the inhale, I will count out six seconds to give you an approximate idea of the pace. At the top of the inhale, I will count to three while you pause or cease breathing. We will then exhale for a count of six, and at the bottom we will pause for a count of three before beginning the next inhale. You will want to stop exhaling before you run out of air so that you are not gasping for breath when you begin the inhale. If you find that you cannot follow the count, go ahead and grab a little air here and there. Are you ready? Let’s practice. Inhale 2-3-4-5-6, pause 2-3, exhale 2-3-4-5-6, pause 2-3. (Repeat two more times and use a timer or clock to count each second)

Now return to relaxed, quiet breathing as you observe what is going on inside your skin. (Pause for 30 seconds) How did you feel when you paused at the end of the breath? (Pause) Did you feel any anxiety? Was there an unwillingness to endure? (Pause) With practice, you might begin to enjoy the pause between breaths as a cessation from all effort and a chance to dwell in the stillness of just being. (Pause for 10 seconds)

Pause. Open your eyes and re-engage with your environment. Notice how breathing in this way massages your internal organs, flexes your thoracic structure, and cleanses your mind. The Three-Part Breath oxygenates your body and trains you to confidently direct your body, even when it is reacting to stress.

Take a deep breath.

0:21 TOOLBOX

We are exploring KF Confession 9:

We learn to withdraw from the Idol Self and begin investing in our True Self.

Last week we talked about using Flow Pages to ask God. It’s so important to ask God about everything. In the past you have practiced the mindless habit of asking your Idol Self about everything. (Repeat) Just as a soldier’s training develops the ability to react automatically in surprising situations, your KF training develops your ability to automatically ask God. Asking God instantly takes your mind away from judgment and virtue stories and helps prevent choices based on compelling needs. Rather than habitually or compulsively reacting out of fear, self-hate, and pride, you can stop and ask God. When you have learned to ask God first, you are on your way to becoming His trustworthy friend.

You have been training hard, learning to automatically react in an oriented way to surprising situations, and you may have had some success in resisting the Idol’s attempts to hijack your life. You might be experiencing a newfound sense of freedom and power. This can feel euphoric, but it’s important to remember this: if the Idol cannot directly derail you, it will find subtle ways to distract you by creating the illusion that you can do more, get away with more, and, perhaps, even fulfill that illusive, idealized glory. When you are running strong, keep in mind that you are still a creature—a very powerful creature, but a creature all the same—living within a certain set of limitations.

While we are asking God to teach us about our real unavoidable limitations, we can also productively choose limitations by simplifying our lives. At first, streamlining our lives within those limitations can require a lot of effort. That extra work often discourages us, so we continue to dissipate our resources in cluttered living. Interior designers understand that simplicity is one of the keys to creating a beautiful space. This kind of simple beauty is harmonious and balanced. Like these interior designers, we can build harmony and balance into our lives by cultivating simplicity. As a result, our lives will be much easier—and more fun. Ask God to show you what is cluttering your life and interfering with the harmony and balance of your True Self. When you get rid of things, activities, and relationships, you create space that draws into your life what is compatible with the person you are becoming. Live in the cycles of life. Trust the process, and let the ebb tide go out, taking with it everything that does not complement the new life that is growing within you. Then allow the tide to return, carrying with it gifts from God and from the Creation.

God is freer to work in you through a simple life. Try withdrawing from an activity or stepping back from relationships that no longer fit your goals. The time, attention, and energy that you give to unnecessary pursuits and unproductive relationships cannot be available for God, His purposes, and for the care of your True Self. There are, of course, some relationships that you can’t let go, but you can let go of your old patterns of handling those relationships. Try fasting from an activity for a week or a month, and then assess its value in your life. Whether you are a mother of small children or the leader of an enterprise, you will be more effective if you simplify. Everyone’s resources are limited, and those resources are there to serve a purpose. You must make choices about which purposes you want them to serve.

Developing a KF routine of self-care requires effort. You have to be intentional about scheduling your training, but don’t try to do everything all at once. Just focus on practicing the KF tools that are working for you. You may find it helpful to schedule your KF work around other established routines. For example, you might meditate for five minutes in the parking lot before you go into work. As with any good investment, if you are diligent and committed, both to your training and to the KF community, you will eventually accrue great returns. In your Flow Pages, explore simplicity. What might a simple life look like for you? What changes could you make? Ask God to teach you to live simply—trimmed for action, ready to respond to His guidance, and able, at a moment’s notice, to joyfully step into the flow and let go.

Our prayer might go something like this:

Savior, unload my soul’s freight that I might live a simple life for You. (Repeat)

Take a deep breath.

1:00 BIBLE TEACHING

Let’s turn again to Romans, chapter 6. In our last lesson, Nancy compared our KF training to an immersion course that is teaching us to live in the new country of karis. The Bible teaches us that the people who live in this new country are no longer afraid of death because their leader, Jesus, has already conquered death.

In the text for this lesson, Paul tells us that we were designed to serve. We cannot help but serve someone. If we abandon one master, we must find another. In our misorientation, we abandoned God and found another master in our Idol Self. We agreed to serve this Idol’s illusions and compulsive needs, and we constructed a way of living that perpetuates the arrogant belief that we can cast off all limitations, do exactly what we please, and still be entitled to excellent outcomes. If we observe our behavior, however, we find that no matter how many virtue stories we tell ourselves about our lives, we are not free. We are compelled by unrecognized inner forces that generate a roller-coaster ride of euphoria and self-hate. Paul is encouraging us to choose the slavery that we were designed for. We were designed to serve God.

This is Nancy’s paraphrase of Romans 6:12–18.

12Therefore, refuse the rule of misorientation in your body—a rule that tyrannizes you into heeding the Idol’s compulsive need to save its life. Your body, as well as the Idol system, is going to die anyway, so there is no point in losing your true life to save it. 13Furthermore, don’t allow your physical body to become a weapon in the service of misorientation, assisting the Idol Self to generate more misorientation. Instead use your physical body as a weapon of orientation for God, assisting Him and His purposes as those who have gone beyond death and fear, and are fully alive. 14In other words, misorientation shall not be your master. Let me explain. You are not living under a legal system, trying to please a misoriented despot. You are living in karis relationship with God and have access to all the privileges of that relationship. 15So now what? Do we continue to choose misorientation because there is no law or despotic master to punish us? Absolutely not! 16Perhaps you haven’t yet figured out that you will be a slave, whether assisting God or the Idol Self. You are the slave of the one that you listen to and obey. It is your choice either to obey misorientation, which produces death, or to obey God, which orients you and the Creation. 17But thank God, you who used to be the slaves of misorientation heeded from your hearts the pattern of instruction that was entrusted to you. 18You were willingly liberated from misorientation and enslaved to orientation.

Take a deep breath.

Paul is letting you know that your slavery is not optional, but your choice of master is. Successful service requires training. Your mission is before you: you are to be a weapon of orientation in this world. As Jesus trains you, He will regularly invite you to sacrifice your Idol’s hope in the Glory of Me! When you start to make that sacrifice, you begin to discover the affirming benefits and ever-developing potential of serving Jesus in your True Self. This discovery does not come simply through learning about meditation techniques or reading the Toolbox and Bible Teaching. You must willingly, humbly, and with effort practice His way of doing things and submit yourself to be trained like a slave.

This is our prayer of commitment:

O Captain, my Captain, make me a weapon of orientation in this world. (Repeat)

Take a deep breath.

1:10 INTEGRATION

Sip some purified water, and you might prepare to take some notes. Assume your meditation posture, and breathe. (Pause for 10 seconds)

Here is our integration focus: Consider becoming a weapon of orientation in this world. (Repeat) (Pause for 30 seconds)

The FAITH Process

Feelings

As you consider becoming a weapon of orientation in this world, recognize any reaction you might feel. Welcome whatever is happening inside your skin, and breathe. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Awareness without judgment

Allow yourself to experience all your thoughts and feelings, without judgment. You might be aware of some resistance or struggle, or you might notice a willingness to explore. (Pause) Welcome your experience and the information it brings to you. Breathe. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Inquiry: What is my body saying?

Bring your attention to your body and notice what it is doing as you consider becoming a weapon of orientation in this world. What sensations are there? (Pause) You might notice what resistance and shutting down or, perhaps, power and trust feel like in your body. (Pause) Gently focus your attention on your body as you consider becoming a weapon of orientation in this world. Breathe. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Talk to Jesus

You might imagine that you are leaning back in the safe and loving arms of Jesus as together you consider becoming a weapon of orientation. Are you comfortable about this possibility, uncomfortable, or are you shutting down? Just observe. (Pause) Have you ever tried serving God in the past? What happened? (Pause) Observe your experience as just information. You might process your experience with a long exhaling breath or the Dragon Breath. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Heal

As you breathe, repeat the word Welcome. (Pause for 10 seconds)

(Pause for 8 seconds or one long breath after each statement)

  • Welcome this opportunity to explore a new possibility.
  • Welcome the Holy Spirit of truth with an open and inquiring mind.
  • Welcome your present reality and the progress you have made.
  • Welcome what you are learning about yourself. (Pause for 10 seconds)

Now, as you breathe, you might use the following statements as a confession to the Lord and turn away from the Idol Self. On the exhale, let go.[1]

(Pause for 5 seconds after each statement)

  • As I become a weapon for God, I let go of being anxious about power and control.
  • As I become a weapon for God, I let go of being anxious about esteem and affection.
  • As I become a weapon for God, I let go of being anxious about what that might entail.
  • As I become a weapon for God, I let go of being anxious about things not going my way. (Pause for 30 seconds)

(Optional) Continue to breathe while I test for further integration.

Take a deep breath.
