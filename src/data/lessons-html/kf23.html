0:08 MINDFULNESS & MEDITATION TRAINING

We sometimes want to avoid the sad truth of KF Confession 5:

We attacked and cruelly suppressed our True Self and lost our true life.

The Self-Hate Massage

Assume a mindfulness posture, and breathe. (Pause) Bring to mind an area of your life that stirs up chronic self-hate for you. Perhaps you cannot please others, or perhaps you cannot please yourself. Perhaps your limitations are interfering with the achievement of your dreams. You may be experiencing self-hate because you have begun to break out of your Idol’s pride system or, perhaps, because you are still stuck in it. If you cannot identify the cause of your self-hate, you might be able to focus on the symptoms. We will bring our hurt and fear into this meditation and get acquainted with it using our KF tools. (Pause for 10 seconds)

CONSIDER

Take a moment to collect yourself within this present space. Feel the air and the clothing on your body. Notice any noises. What is going on inside your skin? (Pause for 30 seconds) Now, consider this area of self-hate. Notice the discomfort you feel when you think of it. Even though you hate this thing, allow it to rest inside your skin for one minute, without judgment, and breathe. (Pause for 1 minute)

COMPASSION

As you breathe, experience yourself as a creature with limitations breathing in a material world. (Pause) You are not a god. (Pause) As you breathe in and out, imagine your area of self-hate moving in and out with the breath, massaging this place in your soul that is stuck or cramped. For one minute, give it and receive it with compassion. (Pause for 1 minute)

CLEANSE

Notice your entire body as it breathes. (Pause) Breathe with your mind, body, and soul as you open the doors of the prison cells of your shame, regrets, hurt, and guilt. (Pause) Allow the clean air to sweep through those dark, stagnant places for one minute. (Pause for 1 minute)

And pause.

Jesus sees our efforts and offers us the gift of an open door to more training and opportunities. He invites us to respond with the karis gift of allowing Him to train and purify us so that we can enter into the joy of fulfilling our potential in karis relationship with Him.

0:20 KARIS CONFESSION

0:21 TOOLBOX

All Idol types feel a compulsive need to terminate the tension that comes with making changes in their lives, and they terminate that tension by returning to old habitual patterns. Although each type experiences and releases their tension in different ways, the driving forces are the same. All types are driven by the fear of self-hate and the fear of their inability to fulfill their idealized dreams. Nancy’s son once told her about a French novelist who described this compulsive need to silence these fears as the rage to conclude.[1] The rage to conclude is the discomfort we feel when we are enduring our craving to return to old habitual patterns. We want to conclude the tension of going into the not knowing, of changing course, and of taking responsibility for the quality of our own lives, so we give in to the compelling needs of our fear and pride.

As you begin to turn toward your True Self, you will regularly feel uneasy and confused about what you should do. That’s because you are disengaging from your Idol’s autopilot, and your claims and shoulds are no longer in charge. The Idol Self won’t give up easily, so you may have chaotic feelings and strong compulsions that you can’t explain. Things may feel worse before they get better even though you are experiencing more stability in your life.

If you are a mastery type and seeking to recover your True Self, you will practice letting go of control and begin resisting the temptation to assert yourself; you will let go of forcing others to comply, to admire, or to meet your standards. You will learn to value understanding others more than being understood. You will come to recognize your own limitations. Even though you may long to be the hero, the punisher, or the Standards and Evaluations Officer, you will start admitting that you are often helpless and vulnerable. Of course, your Idol Self is not interested in becoming a humble beginner who needs to learn new skills, so every time you practice these skills, you will feel quite uncomfortable.

Mastery types have a difficult time attending Karis Fellowships. Showing up regularly means that they need help, have messed up, and can’t figure it all out in a week. Oh, the multitude of horrors they endure! They are even asked to reveal to others their weaknesses and failures! Mastery types crave opportunities to display their superiority; KF, however, invites them to practice humility. Rather than examine the quality of their choices and behavior, they prefer to spend their time asserting themselves in activities that satisfy their pride system. They want to believe that knowing is the same as being and that the illusion of effortless superiority, based on their own evaluation of themselves, is enough to achieve orientation. Mastery types seek to gloriously commandeer the powerful life that is the goal of our KF training. They cannot understand that such a life cannot be gained through willpower or by accumulating information. This higher quality of life is achieved gradually and organically as we continue to develop our True Self—and mastery types find the process exasperating.

Self-effacing people love KF when they first join, but eventually they are confronted with the reality that they must give up their dreams of finding fulfillment through others and take up the very frightening work of investing in their own True Self. If their mission involves anything but self-sacrifice and connecting to others, they feel terrified. Investing in others gives them a sense of value. They need to find their value through others because they don’t value themselves. Whenever they invest in themselves, they suffer the painful backlash of the Idol’s vicious judgment. They often externalize this inner experience and believe that the judgment is coming from other people or organizations—even from Karis Fellowships. They unconsciously goad others into attacking or despising them in order to maintain their role as martyrs and victims. Giving up their role as a self-sacrificing victim would mean giving up their identity.

Resigned people feel uneasy or even anxious about any situation that might reveal their need to change, cooperate, or contribute. They conclude their tension and calm their anxiety by restricting their thinking, feelings, and experiences. They are very adept at stonewalling or excusing themselves with stories that confuse, fatigue, or irritate both themselves and others. It is a huge accomplishment for a resigned person to endure the effort and bother of change and suffer the pain of the associated and unpredictable struggles. Resigned people sometimes leave the Fellowships when they begin to thaw out and start experiencing uncomfortable emotions. They feel anxiety when they realize that they cannot continue to drift but need to grow up and take responsibility for the quality of their lives; they need to circulate and offer something in this world. If they become aware that they need to change, or if they see evidence of real change, they often stop filling out and sharing their Meeting Preparation Guides. They don’t want to deal with real evidence or have any record of beneficial change in their lives. It is inexpressibly difficult for resigned types to work on themselves because they find change terrifying; even the thought of a worksheet or a tap is excruciating. The obvious way to conclude this tension is to quit.

When we are in self-hate, the rage to conclude is compelling, but with the help of KF training, we can just stand and observe. We can also rejoice, knowing that our discomfort is an indication that we are strong enough to engage in battle with the Idol.

In KF lesson 25, we will learn another powerful tool for calming the rage to conclude. It is called the T‑A‑T, or the Tapas Acupressure Technique.[2] If you want to start exploring the T-A-T, you can find instructions on the Karis Fellowships website under the Toolbox tab, or you can search for TAT at Alison’s tapping and TAT videos.

Our prayer might go something like this:

Father, in my rage to conclude, remind me to hold my course toward You. (Repeat)

1:00 BIBLE TEACHING

In this lesson, we will look at some familiar verses from the Gospel of John that give us insight into the mechanism of self-hate. These verses also encourage us to continually present ourselves to the light of examination so that we can learn how to cooperate with God and fulfill our business. Our business is to deal with our fear, reorient to our true glory, and reveal the divine nature that God has given to us, His creatures.

Let’s now read John 3:16–21 in Nancy’s paraphrase. In the preceding verses, Jesus has been talking to Nicodemus about being born again. Poor Nicodemus seems to be feeling some self-hate, and Jesus addresses those feelings.

16God loved this sick world so much that He gave to the world the only child of His nature, His Son, so that everyone who trusts Him with their lives shall not be ruined or destroyed but live an eternal life. 17Let me explain. God did not send His Son into this sick world to decide against the world; God sent His Son so that the world might be rescued through Him. 18There is no judgment against a person who trusts the Son, but those who do not trust Him are already decided against. They are becoming ruined and destroyed because they have not trusted in the character and authority of the only Son of God. 19And this is that judgment: the light has come into this sick world, and people, out of their own character and choice, loved the darkness rather than the light because their beliefs and actions were misoriented, causing pain and trouble for everyone. 20For those who do worthless things hate the light and do not come to the light because they fear that their deeds will be shown to be what they actually are—worthless and producing nothing of real value. 21But the one who is a doer of the truth comes to more and more light so that his or her deeds may be revealed for what they are—the result of hard work and the fulfillment of their own business in God.

Take a deep breath.

Although John often uses the word judgment in this passage, it is evident that God isn’t judging anyone. Quite the contrary! It is we who are judging ourselves. When we studied Neurosis and Human Growth together, we felt exposed. The book shone a light on our illusions and pride, and we may have experienced some symptoms of self-hate, like feeling bored or overwhelmed, or perhaps angry or dismayed. In the same way, when we read about the life of Jesus or compare our choices to those of an oriented person, we judge ourselves and may feel some shame. In the light of oriented understanding, this reaction is quite natural, but we take it a step further—we condemn ourselves. We compare our own misoriented choices to what we now recognize as oriented and healthy, and we find ourselves wanting, or falling short. We might then judge self-examination as bad, reject it, and even hate it. Or we might run away because we simply prefer to go on as we are. So, we see in this passage that self-judgment is really the result of a kind of exposure; we are exposed to light that shows us who we actually are—and we don’t like it.

So, what is John saying here about judgment? Let’s look at this passage in terms of our KF purpose, which is to restore the True Self. As you learn to trust Jesus, you experience His gracious character and stop judging yourself. You are able to rest in His authority as the only Righteous Judge because you know that He is not judging you. You acknowledge that you are His creature, and you understand that your business is to allow yourself to be rescued and to cooperate with Him as, together, you restore your True Self. God has declared you to be safe from condemnation, so it is not your business to heap self-hate on yourself.[3] The more that you understand His authority and your business, the more vision and courage you have to take the Believe It Path instead of judging yourself or others. Jesus is the Great Shepherd, and as you cooperate with Him, He will guide you back to your True Self.

If you do not believe or trust Jesus, you cannot cooperate with Him because, frankly, you are too afraid to do so. You yourself are the only god that you recognize. In your mind, you alone have the authority to judge, and you judge everything, including yourself, according to the standards imposed by your Idol. As a result, you live in a state of constant self-judgment and self-hate. When you are not trusting Jesus, you are trapped in the black box of your pride system. Your mind is attached to what is illusion and what should be rather than open to what is real and what could be. You prefer the darkness of your cherished illusions and rebelliously choose to avoid the light because you do not want the truth about your misorientation to be exposed.

With Jesus as our Advocate in the courtroom of our soul, we have the courage to step into the light and allow our misorientation to be revealed and healed—and as we dare to reveal more and more of who we are, we discover more and more of who He is.[4]

In Karis Fellowships, we have chosen to come to the light in order to have the truth about our deeds revealed. Those deeds may be far from oriented, but what we do with our deeds is quite remarkable and unique: we bring our deeds to the light so that, together with Jesus, we can examine them and find healing. We are no longer living on autopilot, compulsively running away from the light and seeking the comfort and oblivion of darkness. This is a gigantic step forward. In the light, we examine our choices and desires and ask ourselves, “Is this the outcome I want?” If the outcome is not what we want, we set about exploring our healing and begin training hard. We learn to stop repeating the misoriented patterns of the past, and we build new patterns of thought and action that will produce oriented outcomes for our future.

When you are in self-hate about some deed, choice, or some kind of limitation, remember to appreciate the efforts you have made to grow and to change. Your efforts are of extraordinary value for they declare to the whole world the hope of the Gospel—the hope that we can grow and change, that we are not stuck, that we are not slaves. Growth means that we are alive, and anything that is truly alive reveals and declares the glory of God.

Our prayer might go something like this:

Father, kindly lead me to the light so that I might be truly alive. (Repeat)

1:10 INTEGRATION

Sip some clean water and prepare to take some notes. (Pause) Assume a meditation posture, and breathe. (Pause for 10 seconds)

Here is our integration focus: Consider allowing Jesus to reveal your deeds for what they truly are. (Repeat)

Keep in mind that Jesus does not seek to judge you but to rescue and commend you. (Pause for 30 seconds)

The FAITH Process

Feelings

As you consider allowing Jesus to reveal your deeds for what they truly are, how does it feel? (Pause) Your feelings are yours to teach you about yourself, to guide you, and to train you. They can often be uncomfortable. (Pause) Just observe your feelings, and breathe. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Awareness without judgment

Hold your experience in your awareness. You might notice a compulsion to push thoughts or feelings away, to judge them, or to justify them. We often flush feelings or thoughts from our awareness before they have a chance to teach us and stretch us. Gently observe whatever you may be experiencing as you consider allowing Jesus to reveal your deeds for what they truly are. (Pause) Give yourself permission to explore your thoughts and feelings without judgment, and breathe. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Inquiry: What is my body saying?

Bring your attention to your body and notice what it is doing when you consider allowing Jesus to reveal your deeds for what they truly are. What sensations are there? (Pause) Your body is always talking to you. You may not understand its language right now, but you can observe. For example, you might recognize what relief or dismay, or perhaps anxiety or curiosity, feels like in your body. Breathe. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Talk to Jesus

You might imagine that you are leaning back in the safe and loving arms of Jesus as together you consider allowing Him to reveal your deeds for what they truly are. Are you comfortable, uncomfortable, or do you feel numb? Just observe. (Pause) Do you judge the exposure of your deeds as good or evil? (Pause) Are you afraid of something? Do you hope for something? (Pause) Observe your responses as just information, and breathe with a long exhaling breath. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Heal

As you breathe, repeat to yourself the word Welcome. (Pause for 10 seconds)

(Pause for 8 seconds or one long breath after each statement)

  • Welcome this opportunity of an open door inviting you to explore your deeds.
  • Welcome the light of the Holy Spirit, who leads you into all truth.[5]
  • Welcome the reality of where you are right now and how far you have come.
  • Welcome what you are learning about yourself. (Pause for 10 seconds)

Now, as you breathe, you might use the following statements as a confession to the Lord and turn away from the Idol Self. On the exhale, let go.[6]

(Pause for 5 seconds after each statement)

  • As my deeds are revealed, I let go of the craving for power and control.
  • As my deeds are revealed, I let go of the craving for esteem and affection.
  • As my deeds are revealed, I let go of the craving for survival and comfort.
  • As my deeds are revealed, I let go of the craving to change my situation. (Pause for 30 seconds)

(Optional) Continue to breathe while I test for further integration.

Pause. Take a deep breath.
