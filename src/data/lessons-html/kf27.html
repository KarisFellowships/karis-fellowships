0:08 MINDFULNESS & MEDITATION TRAINING

Let’s continue our practice of Centering Prayer.[1]

Assume a meditation posture and breathe. (Pause) Now, in silent prayer, rest in the spirit and the breath. You might practice the balanced breath using the same amount of time for your inhale as for your exhale. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Ask God for a word, and allow that word to surface in your consciousness. (Pause for 30 seconds) Stick to this word during the meditation time. When other thoughts hijack your consciousness, gently return to your word. (Pause for 30 seconds)

For one minute, try recalling the word for one breath and then rest in the stillness for two breaths. (Pause for 1 minute)

And now, as you are able, practice resting in only the stillness, the quiet mind, for one minute. (Pause for 1 minute)

Pause, and take a full deep breath, as if you were stretching your insides. (Pause) Gently open your eyes and re-engage with the meeting.

0:20 KARIS CONFESSION

0:21 TOOLBOX

This month we are focusing our attention on KF Confession 6:

We chose to foster illusions to save the life of our Idol Self.

When we are living out of misorientation, we foster illusions that fall into two categories: the illusion of our perfections and the illusion of our limitations.

In this world, there is real perfection and there is also the illusion of perfection. Real perfection is a quality that cannot be improved or changed. It is finite and not part of our design or purpose. Rather, it is the perfect arrangement of form, like in mathematics or architecture. In contrast to real perfection, imagined perfection is infinite and continually uses the language of always, never, more, and better. Imagined perfection is never satisfied; it chronically requires more and more effort to achieve its illusions. Imagined perfection is the engine of our discontent.[3]

KF members who have seen tremendous improvement in their lives through using the KF tools will sometimes become disenchanted with KF because their accomplishments are not what they imagine they should be. Perhaps they unconsciously imagined an improvement that would support the glory of their Idol Self and became disappointed because they did not achieve it. It’s easy to forget that the Idol will always move the target of our expectations and present new perfections to be discontented about. When you seem to be making no progress through your KF work, you might consider whether you are actively, or unconsciously, discouraging yourself by forgetting what you have already accomplished or by judging any achievement as insignificant and petty. By allowing yourself to dismiss what you have gained, you are inviting the jackals of self-hate to take you down.

Just as there is real perfection and the illusion of perfection, there are also real limitations and the illusion of limitations. Real limitations are set by physical laws like gravity and thermodynamics and by physical resources like time, money, health, or mental and emotional strength. These real limitations are all part of the simple restrictions of living in a material world. There are, of course, other real limitations that have to be honored if we are to care for ourselves, build healthy relationships, and develop a supportive community.

Although our Idol Self refuses to acknowledge real limitations, it carefully fosters imagined limitations—the electric fences and the No Trespassing signs of the Idol’s taboos. If you are a mastery type, you will have taboos about giving up or giving in. You will feel strain when you experience the appearance or the actuality of failure, or when you allow someone else to have the attention or the control. If you are resigned, you will feel the punishing shock of the electric fence when you see yourself changing, feel your real feelings, or engage with real life. It is important to realize that we all have taboos. The taboos of others may differ from ours, and we may not understand them, but taboos have just as much power in other people’s lives as they do in our own.

Think about an area of imagined limitation in your life, a taboo. (Pause for 5 seconds) Your taboo is not a real limitation; it is an area of your life where your reactions, feelings, and thoughts hinder your progress and stifle your ability to appreciate your achievement. Perhaps this taboo prevents you from maintaining your new level of freedom.

We will now use the T-A-T to explore the taboo that you have identified.[4] During the pauses, you might like to jot down a few notes. We will spend one minute on each step, but in your personal work, follow the instructions in the Expanded Version and spend two minutes on each step. You will find the experience to be more thorough and helpful.

The T-A-T

Take the pose by placing your fingers on the three facial points. You might also place the palm of your other hand over the occiput, where your spine meets your skull. Breathe deeply. (Pause)

  1. Experience this imagined and self-imposed taboo or limitation. Breathe. (Pause for 15 seconds) (Repeat the instruction) (Pause for 15 seconds)

Pause. Jot down some thoughts and rate how uncomfortable or resistant you feel about violating this taboo. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Take the pose again and breathe deeply.

  1. Imagine what you would think and feel, and what your life would be like, if you did not have this imagined limitation or taboo. (Pause for 15 seconds) (Repeat the instruction) (Pause for 15 seconds)

Pause and jot down a few notes. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Take the pose again and breathe deeply.

  1. Listen to the clearing statement: “God, thank You for healing the origins of this taboo.” (Pause for 15 seconds) “The origins of this imagined limitation are healing now.” (Pause for 15 seconds)

Pause, and if you wish, write. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Take the pose again, and breathe.

  1. Listen to the clearing statement: “God, thank You for healing all the areas of my life, mind, and body that are misoriented by this imagined limitation.” (Pause for 15 seconds) “All the areas of my life, mind, and body that are misoriented by this taboo are healing now.” (Pause for 15 seconds)

Pause, and write if you wish. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Take the pose again, and breathe.

  1. Listen to the clearing statement: “God, thank You for healing all the areas of my life that get something out of holding on to this taboo.” (Pause for 15 seconds) “All the areas of my life that get something out of holding on to this imagined limitation are healing now.” (Pause for 15 seconds)

Pause. If you wish, jot down a few thoughts. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Take the pose again and breathe deeply.

  1. Listen to the clearing statement: “I forgive everyone and everything that I have blamed for creating this taboo, including God and myself.” Breathe and let go. (Pause for 15 seconds) “I release everyone and everything that I have held responsible for creating this imagined limitation.” (Pause for 15 seconds)

Pause, and write if you wish. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Take the pose again, and breathe deeply.

  1. Listen to the clearing statement: “I ask forgiveness from everyone and everything that I have harmed by holding on to this imagined limitation, including God’s purposes and my True Self.” Breathe. (Pause for 15 seconds) “I accept responsibility for the damage that I have caused to anyone or anything by holding on to this taboo.” Breathe. (Pause for 15 seconds)

Pause. If you wish, jot down your thoughts. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Now write down how you feel about this taboo or limitation. On a scale of one to ten, rate the intensity of your feelings or resistance. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Pause.

Our prayer might go something like this:

Good Shepherd, cut the electric fence of my taboos. (Repeat)

Take a deep breath.

1:00 BIBLE TEACHING

In our last lesson we talked about the illusions created by appearance, talent, and money. We saw how distracted we are by the ATM, and we learned from James that the only real asset is faith—that is, action based on oriented beliefs.[5] We also considered how the ATM can be used to support illusions of effortless superiority and the if onlys of limitless possibilities. For example, we might think, “If only I had enough money, I could stop my body aging.” We are often hoodwinked by the ATM rather than guided by realities.

Many of our perceived limitations are imagined, and in neurosis we carefully foster them in an effort to save the life of our Idol. In chapter 12 of Second Corinthians, we will see how Paul deals with some of his imagined limitations. This passage has encouraged many Christians to revel in, and even be proud of, their weaknesses. Others have used this passage to justify their unwillingness to develop their true potential in Christ. Nancy’s paraphrase of Second Corinthians 12:7–10 is based on her own translation and offers alternative insights. Let’s listen to it now.

7In order to keep me from raising my True Self up to actualization, and because of the loftiness of those revelations about my potential in Christ, I was given a thorn of judgment and self-hate which spotlights all of my human limitations, real or imagined; a messenger that Satan (or the adversary) used to slap me down, so that I could not raise myself up to the height of my true potential. 8Three times I called on the Lord to give me some encouragement about my limitations so that the pain of self-hate and judgment might be sent away from me. 9And the Lord said to me, “My gift of karis relationship is sufficient to reach your goal. Look at it this way. Active energy is matured in frailties. In other words, lack of skill and weaknesses are not to be avoided for fear of self-hate but, rather, to be acknowledged and trained so that your real abilities may have more effect with less effort.” 10With greatest pleasure, therefore, I have confidence in my real weaknesses rather than in my imagined strengths because the strategic can-do power of Christ that dwells in me is waiting to develop real strength from those weaknesses. Because of Christ’s ability to train me, I have a positive attitude in weakness, in self-hate, in necessities, in persecution, and whenever I am in a tight spot on behalf of Christ. It’s like this: when I feel weak in my Idol Self, at the same time I, in my True Self, am very capable.

Take a deep breath.

Rather than imagining that Satan, the Idol Self, and life events have the power to limit him, Paul takes the position of regarding all life experiments as opportunities to train. He has learned to let go of making his limitations the excuse for remaining in the dreary pasture of his Idol’s comfort zone. He exults in his weaknesses, trusting in Christ’s ability to train him in real skills and a growing God-confidence. Rather than going into self-hate each time an area of weakness is exposed, Paul welcomes weakness as an opportunity to explore and grow. Having a need for training is not a limitation—it is a gift. Paul is eager to courageously explore his weaknesses and to give God the opportunity to help him discover the strengths inherent in his design. In the process of becoming strong, Paul breaks through the electric fence of his imagined limitations and discovers his real limitations. This ongoing discovery strengthens his self-confidence in every area of his life.

All real growth is stressful. Let’s hear that again. All real growth is stressful. You cannot pick up true glory on the bargain table—there are no shortcuts to developing strength and maturity. That’s why very few people are interested in real growth. In order to grow, we must move out of the comfortable land of predictable miseries and unsatisfying pleasures and courageously step into the stresses of wandering into the not-knowing with untried skills, but with hope. Through the experiment of navigating this unfamiliar territory, we gain confidence and our faith is strengthened. All the ground that we cover in our explorations becomes familiar territory. Experiments become experience, and life is no longer scary because we have real, tested skills. The struggle against the internal resistance of imagined limitations can be extremely productive. In that struggle we grow strong; we develop real tested skills that God and everyone else can trust.

Let God, your Trainer, decide when it is time for you to rest and when it is time for you to continue the journey. He will not push you beyond your capabilities, but He is eager to see you grow. As you continually stretch both your internal and external capacities, you grow from strength to strength, from faith to faith, from trust to trust, and from glory to glory. Each time that you are launched into a new challenge, you will begin the journey with more hope and courage than you had in previous challenges. You cannot be perfect and perform perfectly in new challenges because you are an eternally developing creature who is always learning. Perfection is finite, but the karis relationship with God is infinite.

Our prayer might go something like this:

My Divine Trainer, I am more interested in karis with You than in my imagined limitations. (Repeat)

Take a deep breath.

1:10 INTEGRATION

Sip some purified water and, if you wish, prepare to take some notes. Assume a meditation posture, and breathe. (Pause for 10 seconds)

Here is our integration focus: Consider giving God permission to expose a taboo and to train you beyond that imagined limitation. (Repeat) (Pause for 30 seconds)

The FAITH Process

Feelings

As you consider giving God permission to expose a taboo and to train you, breathe and recognize any feelings or resistance that you may be experiencing. (Pause) Your feelings are yours to teach you about yourself, to guide you, and to train you. They can often be uncomfortable. Recognize with compassion whatever you are experiencing, and breathe. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Awareness without judgment

Allow whatever you are experiencing to rest in your awareness as you consider giving God permission to expose a taboo and to train you. Observe your experience. Feelings and passing thoughts are not right or wrong; they just are. Give yourself permission to explore them without judgment, and breathe. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Inquiry: What is my body saying?

Bring your attention to your body and notice what it is doing when you consider giving God permission to expose a taboo and train you. You might acknowledge to yourself what sensations are in your body right now. (Pause) Observe those sensations, and breathe. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Talk to Jesus

You might imagine that you are leaning back in the safe and loving arms of Jesus as together you consider this taboo and this opportunity to train. Are you comfortable or uncomfortable? Just observe. (Pause) Do you feel compelled to judge exposure and the need for training as good or evil? (Pause) What story do you want to tell about this limitation or taboo? What story do you want to tell about training? (Pause) Observe your responses as just information. You might breathe with a long exhaling breath or with the Dragon Breath.[6] (Pause for 30 seconds)

Heal

As you breathe, repeat to yourself the word Welcome. (Pause for 10 seconds)

(Pause for 8 seconds or one long breath after each statement)

  • Welcome this opportunity to explore your taboos.
  • Welcome the Holy Spirit who stands ready with His spiritual snippers to cut your electric fence.
  • Welcome where you are today and the progress you have made.
  • Welcome what you are learning about yourself. (Pause for 10 seconds)

Now, as you breathe, you might use the following statements as a confession to the Lord and turn away from the Idol Self. On the exhale, let go.[7]

(Pause for 5 seconds after each statement)

  • In the face of this taboo, I let go of my longing for power and control.
  • In the face of this taboo, I let go of my longing for esteem and affection.
  • In the face of this taboo, I let go of my longing for security and comfort.
  • In the face of this taboo, I let go of my longing for perfection. (Pause for 30 seconds)

(Optional) Continue to breathe while I test for further integration.

Pause. Take a deep breath.
