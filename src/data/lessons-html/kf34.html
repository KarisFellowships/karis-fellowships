0:21 TOOLBOX

In KF Confession 8 we commit to partnering with God in the rescue of our True Self:

We co-labor with the Creator to seek and to save that which was lost.

This month we are learning about the CORE muscles of our soul. CORE—C-O-R-E—stands for Courage, Orientation, Reality, Endurance. Last week we looked at how life experiments offer effective training opportunities to grow our courage. When we walk through suffering or success mindfully, we develop the muscle of our courage, which increases our confidence to explore other challenges—challenges that we know will bring more suffering and, also, more success. Challenges stretch our courage, our ability to remain oriented, our engagement with reality, and our endurance.

When you build a muscle at the gym, you work against some kind of resistance, and when you build the muscle of courage, the resistance you work against is fear. Courage is not the absence of fear; it is choosing to act in an oriented manner when fear is goading you to return to habitual and misoriented ways of relieving anxiety. Think of it this way: every real virtue is developed in the face of fear, courage is the soil in which every virtue is planted, and endurance gives every virtue time to grow.

Your Idol likes to say, “I did it my way,” but actually doing it my way only means that you reacted to the fear of death with your habitual patterns—that is, you asserted your claims, met the demands of your shoulds, and maintained your pride system. My way is no way at all. It is the hamster wheel that uses up your resources but goes nowhere. We have all seen people who do their own thing, offer only feeble contributions to the community, and never make substantial progress in their lives. These people end up becoming chaotic, demanding, or listless. By contrast, those who have trained to live lives of courageous virtue experience spontaneous love, joy, and peace and have access to all kinds of marvelous gifts.

With growing courage, we can learn to reach for orientation in more areas of our lives. Acting in oriented ways is sometimes called obedience. The idea of obedience might be repulsive to you, so you may feel rebellious right now. That is understandable because many of us have experienced abuse in the name of obedience. Authorities in our lives did not consider our needs or desires. They just imposed on us their compulsive need for control. On the other hand, some of us were never taught to obey, and from that neglect we learned that obedience is bad, unnecessary, or too hard—something that demands too much effort and offers no reward. When God tells us to obey, however, He is giving us a gift. He is not impoverishing us or using us. He is training us to be free. To obey God is to recognize that we are creatures who thrive when we live according to our design. God is our Designer, and He knows how we work and what works for us. Trust Him to bring into your life what will train and build your True Self for fulfillment and freedom.

Orientation is living in harmony with God and with the Creation. This Creation includes your body, your personality, your mission, and, also, your community. The Creation flows within the limitations of its design, and as we work within the limitations of our design, our lives line up with the flow of life. Oriented people are ecosystems that are spiritually and energetically cleansing themselves; and by cleansing themselves, they cleanse their environment. We have all been polluted by toxic people. Perhaps you are related to some of them. Cleaning up the ecosystem of your family may be your most important mission in this world.

Just as there are rules in the darkroom for developing film, so there are rules in life for developing your True Self.[2] By choosing to obey these rules, you clean up your life, and you help clean up the energetic path of those who went before you. You also provide a cleaner environment for those who follow you. So, when you read the Bible, look for examples of do’s and don’ts and try not to fear this guidance. Telling stories about how restrictive or hard that guidance is won’t benefit you or anyone else. Instead, recognize the hope that this guidance brings.

Now that you know how to manage your self-hate, you can courageously face whatever reality your choices reveal about your faith, the quality of your orientation, and your commitment to your own growth and mission. You can then valiantly choose to reorient to an authentic life, seeking and saving what has been lost. All that you learn in this process will be woven into the fabric of your real glory. The Father of glory wants to glorify you in your True Self, the self that He designed, so that you might glorify Him. You will discover that the recovery of what you have lost will become part of your message to those in this sick world who have lost the same thing. Give up the Idol and its compulsive illusions about who you should be. No matter what has happened to you, your True Self is still there, waiting to be discovered, nurtured, and supported while you press on to real glory.

Our prayer might go something like this:

Lord Jesus, support me as I make oriented choices to nurture my True Self and reveal my true glory. (Repeat)

1:00 BIBLE TEACHING

Heaven rejoices when we start to practice KF Confession 8: [3]

We co-labor with the Creator to seek and to save that which was lost.

Our Bible passages for this lesson are taken from chapter 15 of the Gospel of John and chapter 3 of First Peter. Let’s start with Nancy’s paraphrase of selected verses from John 15:1–11. Jesus is teaching about the relationship between the True Vine and its branches.

1I and no other am the True Vine, and my Father is the vinedresser. 2All the branches attached to Me that are not bearing fruit He takes away, and all the branches that are bearing fruit He prunes back so that they can carry more fruit . . . 4In all of your activities, remain in Me, even as I am remaining in you. Just as the branch must remain connected to the vine in order to bear fruit, so you must remain connected to Me . . . 8When you become My disciples and bear much fruit, others benefit and they honor My Father. 9Just as the Father is well-pleased with Me, I am well-pleased with you. Live in My love. 10If you guard My commands, you will remain in My love, just as I have guarded My Father’s commands and remain in His love. 11I have told you these things so that you might experience My joy and that your joy might be complete.

Take a deep breath.

Jesus is the True Vine

Jesus calls Himself the True Vine. This implies that there is a false vine, and Nancy would call this false vine the Idol Self. The True Self is a branch connected to the True Vine, but sometimes that branch doesn’t seem to be producing much fruit. Have you ever seen a grapevine that’s been pruned for winter? At this dormant stage of its life cycle, the vine is unproductive and unattractive. From a distance the vine looks dead, and you would swear that it could never produce fruit again. You might feel that way about yourself. But if you understand the growth cycle of the vine, you will know that the vine is not dead. It is quietly enduring the winter while it secretly and mysteriously recharges and prepares to produce abundant fruit in the spring.

We glorify the Father by bearing much fruit

The Fruit of the Spirit and our oriented choices can be seen by others.[4] When people see this fruit in us, they recognize the character of Jesus, who is the True Vine and the material expression of the Father. We honor God our Father by remaining connected to the Vine and by bearing fruit in its season.

We abide in the love of Jesus through obedience

The circulating life force of the True Vine is love. If we remain in the Vine, we have access to this love, which, like breath, flows in and through us in harmony with God and the Creation. In this state of harmony, we can do astonishing things. How do we stay attached to Jesus, the True Vine? We stay attached to the True Vine by abiding in His love, and we abide in His love through obedience.

Now let’s flip over to First Peter 3:8–12, which we will read in Nancy’s paraphrase.

8Here is a summary of what I’ve just been saying: All of you, be like loving family— harmonious, compassionate, tender-hearted, and humble in spirit. 9Get into a healthy cycle of blessing others and inheriting a blessing instead of perpetuating the destructive cycle of law-keepers, who return harm for harm and restore their hurt pride by punishing others. Your choice to bless others depends on your karis relationship with God; it does not depend on what others do or don’t do. 10Do you remember Psalm 34? “Those who desire to live well—to love and to experience good days—reveal that intention by refusing to speak the harm on the tip of their tongues and by refusing to use polite words to cloak resentful wrath. Those who choose life take responsibility for their feelings. They choose to swerve away from harming the Creation, and they do good to themselves and to others. They seek peace and pursue it like a hunter. 12Why do they choose to live this way? Because the eyes of the Lord are toward the oriented and His ears attend to their prayer, but the face of the Lord is actively against those who perpetuate misorientation.”

Take a deep breath.

God has invited you into a karis relationship with Him so that you can inherit His blessing. You can’t inherit His blessing if you continue to live life your own way—taking cheap, habitual shortcuts to relieve your anxiety and save your Idol’s pride. By consistently following Peter’s short list of do’s and don’ts, you continue to invest in your stock portfolio of blessing, which in due season will produce abundant and glorious fruit.

Our prayer might go something like this:

Lord of blessing, whatever it takes, bring me into my inheritance of blessing. (Repeat)

Take a deep breath.

1:10 INTEGRATION

Sip some purified water, and you might prepare to take some notes. Assume your meditation posture, and breathe. (Pause for 10 seconds)

Here is our integration focus: Consider an area of your life where you might invest to inherit a blessing. (Repeat) (Pause for 30 seconds)

The FAITH Process

Feelings

As you consider an area of your life where you might invest to inherit a blessing, how does it feel? (Pause) Our feelings are ours to teach us about ourselves, to guide us, and to train us. They can often be uncomfortable. (Pause) Recognize with compassion whatever you are experiencing, and breathe. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Awareness without judgment

Allow whatever you are experiencing to rest in your awareness. Observe whatever tension you may be feeling as you consider where you might invest to inherit a blessing. You might notice a compulsion to push away or excuse thoughts and feelings, or to make up a virtue story. (Pause) Feelings and passing thoughts are not right or wrong; they just are. Give yourself permission now to explore them without judgment, and breathe. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Inquiry: What is my body saying?

Bring your attention to your body and notice what it is doing when you consider this area of your life where you might invest to inherit a blessing. (Pause) You might notice what an old pattern of rationalizing or an old story feels like in your body. You might feel compulsive strain, pushing, and craving or, perhaps, the fear of loss. Gently keep your attention focused on whatever you might be sensing. Breathe. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Talk to Jesus

You might imagine that you are leaning back in the safe and loving arms of Jesus as together you consider this area of your life where you might invest to inherit a blessing. Does that feel pleasant, unpleasant, or vague and slippery? Just observe. (Pause) What do you believe when you consider this investment? What story do you want to tell? (Pause) Are you afraid of something—of yourself, of others, or of loss? Perhaps you feel hope. Observe your experience as just information, and breathe. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Heal

God created all of us to have feelings, and for thousands of years human beings have felt like you feel when they have considered making an investment to inherit future blessing. You can stop telling stories about this investment by asking yourself, “Are my present investment choices reflecting who I really am in Christ? How might I invest today in a future of blessing?” (Pause) With compassion for yourself and for others who are making this choice to invest, breathe and help your body process with a long exhaling breath. (Pause for 30 seconds)

(Optional) Continue to breathe while I test for further integration.

Pause. Take a deep breath.
