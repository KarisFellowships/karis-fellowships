0:08 MINDFULNESS & MEDITATION TRAINING

This month we will be learning about the self-hate experience. You might think that your enemy is self-hate, but that is not true. Your real enemy is the Idol Self. Self-hate is simply the result of living in the Idol’s system. When you fail to meet your idealized standards, the Idol uses self-hate to drive you into compulsive choices and then punishes you with more self-hate for having made those choices. Over the last few weeks, we have been retraining our minds and learning to value our real or imagined failures as opportunities for growth. We have also been exploring tools that help us deal with the associated feelings, motivations, and behaviors of self-hate. The training isn’t easy, but we are training hard so that we can fight easy for the life of our True Self. Even in the midst of self-hate we can connect to God and make oriented choices.

Research has proven the many positive benefits of meditation and mindfulness practice.[1] One of those benefits is the ability to recognize self-hate. Mindfulness training helps us pause, step back, and observe rather than react. The ability to step back gives us the breathing space that we often need to make a healthier choice.

The Self-Hate Massage

Assume a mindfulness posture, and breathe. (Pause for 10 seconds) Bring to mind an area of your life that chronically stirs up self-hate for you. Perhaps you have made some mistakes, or perhaps your limitations have prevented you from achieving something. You might dislike your physical body, or you might wish that you were someone else. Let’s connect that hurt to our breathing and get acquainted with it using our new tools.

CONSIDER

Take a moment to collect yourself within your present space. Feel the air or the clothing on your skin. Notice any noises. (Pause for 30 seconds) Now consider this area of self-hate. Notice the discomfort you feel when you think of it. Even though you hate this thing, gently and without judgment allow it to rest inside your skin for one minute. Breathe. (Pause for 1 minute)

COMPASSION

As you breathe, experience yourself as a creature with limitations breathing in a material world. (Pause) You are not a god. (Pause) As you breathe in and out, imagine your area of self-hate moving in and out with the breath, massaging this place in your soul that is stuck or cramped. For one minute, help that area of self-hate circulate in and out with the breath. Give it and receive it with compassion. (Pause for 1 minute)

CLEANSE

Notice your entire body as it breathes. (Pause) Breathe with your mind, body, and soul as you open the doors of doors of the prison cells of your shame, regrets, pain, and guilt. (Pause) Allow the clean air to sweep through those dark, stagnant places for one minute. (Pause for 1 minute)

And pause.

Our hymn for this confession is My Faith Looks Up to Thee.

May Thy rich grace impart

Strength to my fainting heart,

My zeal inspire;

As Thou hast died for me,

Oh, may my love to Thee

Pure, warm, and changeless be,

A living fire.[2]

Take a deep breath.

0:21 TOOLBOX

This month we are exploring KF Confession 5:

We attacked and cruelly suppressed our True Self and lost our true life.

Let’s consider these five points about self-hate:

Our job is to grow out of our self-hate

This month we will begin looking at how self-hate operates in our lives. We have two choices when dealing with self-hate: we can either appease it by sacrificing our True Self and submitting to the demands of the Idol, or we can grow out of it.

Train hard–fight easy

When we experience self-hate, we often don’t know what we are experiencing. We just suddenly feel the need to run back to old feelings, stories, and behaviors that have made us feel safe or comfortable in the past. We can’t deal with self-hate without training. This is why it is imperative that we train hard. It’s not our goal as God’s people to repeatedly cause harm and create chaos. Our goal is to produce the Fruit of the Spirit in every situation.[3] To do that, we need to train hard so that we can fight self-hate easy.

Calm the fear through mindfulness training

Over the years we have learned to fear self-hate and the ravaging chaos it creates. What’s more, we have developed automatic and habitual patterns of thought and behavior that we hope will enable us to avoid our self-hate. Rather than reverting to these habitual patterns, we can use mindfulness to calm our minds and bodies. We are then free to choose healthy patterns of thought and behavior that help us grow out of our self-hate.

Just as mindfulness training helps us refocus our wandering minds during meditation, it empowers us to redirect our thinking when the Idol has us by the throat and is lashing us with self-hate. When we recognize the symptoms of self-hate, we can choose to simply wait and mindfully endure our chaotic feelings until we are able to figure out what’s going on. We learn that we do not have to react. Instead, we can compassionately observe, listen, and learn.

Writing the lessons for this confession was very difficult for Nancy because for most of her life she had been paralyzed by self-hate. She felt terrified and wanted to go into paralysis and procrastination to affirm her compulsive need to feel inadequate and a disappointment both to herself and to others. She wrote for a whole week and was unable to find her path.

Self-hate can actually strengthen our endurance

Self-hate has the power to stop us in our tracks, so how was Nancy able to write this lesson? She had been in training through the book study and through meditation practice. She persevered in her work despite her discomfort, working against the resistance of her fear. This work-out strengthened her courage and trained her to endure skillfully. She was able to choose healthy and productive behaviors no matter how she felt. The week of writing and getting nowhere actually led her somewhere. She learned how to be productive in the midst of her self-hate, knowing that God would support her if she humbly endured her discomforts.

The unlived life is not worth examining

Socrates, the ancient Greek philosopher, said that the unexamined life is not worth living,[4] but some writers turn that quotation around and assert that the unlived life is not worth examining. (Repeat) We have lived in a state of perpetual flight from self-hate for so long that flight feels natural to us. Running away may help us feel less tension in the moment, but that choice is compulsive rather than free and does not express the glory of God. Running away results in an unlived life. The examination of such a life might awaken a little pity, but it will also be extremely boring.

Flight from self-hate looks different for different Idol types. Some will run away by controlling or abusing themselves or others; some fly by appeasing and pleasing; and then there are those who flee by taking refuge in their fortress of non-engagement. All neurotic flight is self-sabotage. Think of any good story. It’s the discovery of the hero’s character, how he or she grows and responds to life, that grips our interest and helps us relate to the hero. But what if the story was a constant series of “This scary thing happened, and he ran away. And then this other painful thing happened, and she ran away. And then there was this incredible opportunity and . . .”? By now we can finish the story for ourselves—we ran away.

With training, you can learn to stand without running away. You can mindfully stay inside your skin as the wave of self-hate rushes over you and eventually exhausts itself on the shore.

Our prayer might go something like this:

Captain of the hosts, train me to stand. (Repeat)

1:00 BIBLE TEACHING

KF Confession 5 describes the path of wasted energy and lost potential:

We attacked and cruelly suppressed our True Self and lost our true life.

Let’s look at Psalm 23:1–4. Nancy offers here a valid alternative translation using familiar KF vocabulary.

1The Lord is my Shepherd,

I have everything that I need today.

2He makes me lie down satisfied in green pastures;

He leads me beside quiet waters.

3For His own reputation and for my reputation as His creature,

He restores and repairs my True Self.

4He guides me in the tracks of orientation even though

I walk through the valley of the shadow of death.

I fear no harm, for You are with me.

The guidance and training of Your rod and staff comfort me.

Take a deep breath.

Our Shepherd is the Lord, not our Idol Self

Our relationship with the Shepherd is not a legal contract based on shoulds, claims, and punishments. It is based on the trust and the trustworthiness that is developed in a karis relationship. The Shepherd takes into consideration your unique personhood as He guides you; He responds to what you reveal about yourself. He is the one who trains you for your joy and for your mission.

I have everything that I need for today

With Jesus as our Shepherd, and not the Idol Self, we can gradually let go of fighting for our idealized life, fleeing self-hate, and trying to get our compulsive needs met. As we spend time doing things His way, we begin to experience satisfaction and contentment.

One of the things that we need when we are in self-hate is a quiet, safe place. When you are with someone who is suffering from self-hate, Nancy’s advice is this: No sudden movements. No loud noises. (Repeat) In other words, be compassionate around those in self-hate, including yourself—and be very careful.

He restores my True Self, not my Idol Self

In order to restore our lost True Self, we need to calm down, follow the Shepherd’s leading, and find rest. The Hebrew word for restore can be translated as turning back. It also carries the meaning of repair. Does your soul need repair? The soul that Jesus died for and repairs is our True Self, not the Idol Self. Sometimes we feel very disappointed about that.

He guides me in the tracks of orientation for His name’s sake

If we call ourselves Christians, we bear the name of Jesus and are responsible for His reputation. As oriented people, we are the representatives of His name and character in this messed-up world. Sometimes we get caught up in representing a way of thinking or an organization. That is not our real business. Our business is to be oriented to God and represent His kingdom.

The tracks of orientation lead into the valley of the shadow of death

To our dismay, the tracks of orientation always lead into the valley of the shadow of death. It will feel like death to give up old patterns and behaviors that you have habitually used to save your Idol’s life.[5] We are in Karis Fellowships because we want a different life, but in order to gain that different life, we have to lose the Idol’s life. The fear of death is only a shadow for Christians. It is the darkness that we enter when we dare to resist the Idol Self and its pride system. The fear of death is real, but it moves on like a cloud passing over the sun. It only has the power of a shadow.

His rod and staff comfort me

The rod and staff are instruments of training—the do’s and don’ts of the Bible. They replace the shoulds and claims of your own legal systems. When you commit to doing things God’s way, you have to stop doing things your way. That feels like losing your life. But when you want to abandon the path toward orientation and avoid this difficult process of training, the promise of God’s presence and His faithful provision can help you keep going. You can trust the rod and staff in the Shepherd’s hands as He trains you for your potential.

Psalm 23 encourages us to grow in our courage while we confront the problem of our pride, self-hate, and judgments. With God’s support, we can journey productively through any problem.

Our prayer might go something like this:

Shepherd, strengthen my courage and endurance for the valley. (Repeat)

Take a deep breath.

1:10 INTEGRATION

Sip some purified water and prepare to take some notes. Assume a mindfulness posture, and breathe. (Pause for 10 seconds)

Here is our integration focus: Consider something in your life that feels like the valley of the shadow of death. (Repeat) (Pause for 30 seconds)

The FAITH Process

Feelings

How does it feel to stand on the edge of the valley of the shadow of death? (Pause) Your feelings are yours to teach you about yourself, to guide you, and to train you. They can often be uncomfortable. (Pause) You can learn to recognize and honor your feelings without allowing them to master you. Recognize with compassion whatever you are experiencing, and breathe. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Awareness without judgment

Allow your experience to rest in your awareness as you consider this situation that feels like the valley of the shadow of death. Observe whatever tension you may be feeling. (Pause) Give yourself permission now to explore your feelings and thoughts without judgment, and breathe. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Inquiry: What is my body saying?

Bring your attention to your body and notice what it is doing when you consider this situation. What sensations are there? (Pause) If your sensations are uncomfortable, you can move your attention to an area of your body that is not reacting, and rest. When you are ready, return to respectfully observe those uncomfortable sensations. Breathe. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Talk to Jesus

You might imagine that you are leaning back in the safe and loving arms of Jesus as together you consider this situation that feels like the valley of the shadow of death. Do you feel compelled to judge that valley as good or evil? (Pause) What do you believe about this valley? What story do you want to tell? (Pause) How might your Idol’s pride be hurt if you were to step into the valley? (Pause) Observe your responses as just information, and breathe with a long exhaling breath. (Pause for 30 seconds)

Heal

God created us to have all of these feelings, and for thousands of years human beings have been feeling like you feel when they have considered a valley that feels like death. You are not alone in your experience. In the Bible we read of many people who have walked through dark valleys. (Pause) Feelings are just chemical reactions in your body, and that chemistry is often created by your past experiences.[6] Just acknowledging your feelings can help you understand yourself. When feelings bring you information, you can choose what you will do with that information. (Pause) We must each contend with our own valleys, so breathe and allow some compassion to flow toward yourself and toward others. Help your body process with a long exhaling breath. (Pause for 15 seconds)

Allow your feelings to inform you but not motivate you. Apply your training, consider what is your business, and make oriented choices as you enter your valley. (Pause for 15 seconds)

(Optional) Continue to breathe while I test for further integration.

Pause. Take a deep breath.
